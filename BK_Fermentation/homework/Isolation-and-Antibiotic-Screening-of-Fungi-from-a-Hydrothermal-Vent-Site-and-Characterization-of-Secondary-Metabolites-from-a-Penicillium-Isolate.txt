Abstract Five new compounds were isolated from
Penicillium sp. Y-5-2 including an austin derivative 4, four
isocoumarins 9, 11, 12, and 13, together with two known
isocoumarins 8 and 10, and six known austin derivatives 1,
2, 3, 5, 6, and 7 and one phenol 14. Their structures and
relative configurations were established by spectroscopic
means. The absolute configurations of 4, 11, and 13 were
defined mainly by comparison of quantum chemical TDDFT
calculated and experimental ECD spectra. The cyclization of
the pentan-2-ol pendant at C-3 in compound 13 allowed the
assignment of a new 2,3,4,4a,6,10b-hexahydro-1H-benzo[c]chromene isocoumarin skeleton. New compounds 9, 11, and
13 revealed inhibitory activities against E. coli at MIC values
around 32 μg/mL. The known compound 14 showed potent
antibiotic activity against Staphylococcus aureus and Bacillus
subtilis with MIC values 8 and 2 μg/mL, respectively, with no
cytotoxicity when tested in vitro. A rapid and efficient

Introduction
Marine microorganisms, especially marine fungi, were proven
to be a rich source of novel biologically active compounds
(Newman and Cragg 2012; Sureram et al. 2013; Saleema
et al. 2007). Marine-derived fungi residing in extreme conditions like hydrothermal vents with unique temperatures, pressure, and elevated concentration of heavy metals are now becoming the focal point of scientists (Jiang et al. 2013; Xu
2015). Despite the difficulty of isolating hydrothermal vents
marine fungi, previous reports have shown different culture
techniques under standard laboratory conditions which
allowed their successful cultivation (Wilson and Brimble
2009). Diverse compounds such as saccharides, terpenoids,
and alkaloids have effectively been isolated from these microorganisms where new potent secondary metabolites are becoming more and more common and abundant (Pettit 2011).
Four new alkaloids, meleagrin D and E and roquefortine H
and I, were isolated from a 5080-m-deep-ocean sedimentderived fungus Penicillium sp. along with two known compounds, namely, meleagrin B and conidiogenone C, which
were revealed to be cytotoxic against cancer cell lines (Du
et al. 2010). Moreover, three new bioactive breviane
spiroditerpenoids were isolated from the fungi Penicillium
sp. at a depth of 5115 m under standard conditions using
conventional fungal PDA media in seawater where two of

Mar Biotechnol

them could be the precursor of an unexplained skeleton (Li
et al. 2009).

Materials and Methods
General Experimental Procedures
Optical rotations were measured in MeOH on a Perkin-Elmer341 polarimeter. The IR spectra were run on a Nicolet Avatar
360 FT-IR spectrometer. 1H NMR (500 MHz) and 13C NMR
(125 MHz) spectra were measured at 25 °C on a Bruker
AVANCE DMX 500 NMR spectrometer with TMS as internal
standard. UV spectra were also recorded in MeOH on a
Shimadzu UV2550. CD spectra were measured on a JASCO
J-715 (JASCO) circular dichroism spectrometer. ESIMS were
recorded on an Agilent 1260-6230 LCMS and an Agilent
6460 Triple Quad LC/MS. TLC was performed using precoated plates (Silica gel 60 F254, Haiyang, Qingdao, China) of
0.25 mm thickness. Prep. HPLC was performed on an Agilent
1100 system equipped with a Venusil MP C18 column
(10 mm × 250 mm, Agela Technologies, Tianjin, China).
Origin and Isolation of Fungi
Aspergillus sp. YQ-1, Penicillium sp. YQ-5, Penicillium sp.
Y-5-1, Penicillium sp. Y-5-2, and Acremonium sp. HS-1 were
isolated from the hydrothermal vent sediment collected in
May 2014, from Kueishantao off Taiwan. Kueishantao
Island, a young volcanic island that is located on the western
margin of Kuroshio and 10 km offshore northeastern Taiwan,
is on the extension of the most western Ryukyu arc and part of
the Okinawa Trough (Peng et al. 2011). A cluster of many
small shallow hydrothermal vents close to the eastern
Kueishantao Island emit various gases and hydrothermal fluid. The fluid has been measured with pH of about 2 and temperatures above 116 °C, and presence of various chemical
substances, such as CH4, HS−, and many trace metals, have
been noted (Chen et al. 2005).
There are two typical kinds of hydrothermal vent in this
area: yellow vents and white vents (Chen et al. 2005). Because
the yellow vents discharge high content of elemental sulfur
particles, we specifically aimed to collect the sulfur-rich sediment of the yellow vents. A typical higher-temperature vent,
the Byellow spring^ (vent H, location 24.83489° N,
121.96210° E, depth 8 m) was selected for this research.
The scuba divers took self-made samplers to the vent mouth
(116 °C) and filled the samplers with sulfur-rich sediment.
One gram of sediment was homogenized using a blender containing 20 mL sterile seawater. The homogenate was further
diluted with sterile seawater (1:10, 1:100, 1:1000, 1:10,000)
where 200 μL of each dilution was inoculated in triplicate
onto Petri dishes containing PDA (10.0 g glucose, 20.0 g

potato extract, 15 g agar per liter artificial seawater). The
plates were incubated in the clean bench at 25 °C for several
days until the morphology of the fungi could be distinguished.
Aspergillus sp. XZ-1, Aspergillus versicolor XZ-2, and
Aspergillus versicolor XZ-4 were isolated from the intestine
of the hydrothermal vent crab Xenograpsus testudinatus, one
of the rare organisms residing in the hydrothermal vent field.
Xenograpsus testudinatus was discovered and identified as a
new species in 2000 (Ng et al. 2000), which is the only macro
crustacean species that can live in this low-pH, high-sulfide,
and metal-rich Kueishantao marine environment.
The surface of the crab sample was rinsed with sterile artificial seawater thrice. The crab’s intestine was excised with a
sterile scalpel and scraped into powder which was afterwards
homogenized using a blender containing 20 mL sterile seawater. The homogenate was diluted with sterile seawater (1:5,
1:25, 1:125, 1:625) where 200 μL of each was spread on agar
plate (PDA) and finally incubated at 25 °C.
Identification and Cultivation of Fungi
18S ribosomal RNA gene was used to identify the strains.
Sequences were then used in BLAST search listed in
Table S3. Strain Y-5-2 showed a similarity of 99% to sequences obtained from Penicillium sp. KU350747 and
Penicillium pinophilum isolate MR-2 18S ribosomal RNA
gene (Ding et al. 2016; Sahare et al. 2012). Therefore, strain
Y-5-2 was identified as a Penicillium sp. (GenBank:
KY620115) The fungus was cultured under static conditions
at 28 °C in 500-mL Erlenmeyer flasks containing 200 mL of
liquid culture medium, composed of glucose (10.0 g/L), potato extract (20.0 g/L), and artificial seawater (35 g sea salt in
1 L of distilled H2O). After 3 weeks of cultivation, 40 L of
whole broth was filtered to separate the mycelium. After fermentation, the medium was extracted with EtOAc.
Extraction and Isolation
Forty liters of the fungal culture broth was filtered. The airdried thallus part was extracted at room temperature with
MeOH (3 × 1 L), respectively. The extracts were evaporated
in vacuo to afford a gummy residue. The residue was dissolved in H 2 O (250 mL) and extracted with EtOAc
(3 × 500 mL). The broth (40 L) was extracted with EtOAc
(2 × 40 L). Both were combined and concentrated to give an
organic extract (5.80 g). The extract was subjected to liquid
chromatography over a silica gel column using a gradient
elution of petroleum ether (PE)-EtOAc-MeOH (PE/
EtOAc = 1:0, 20:1, 10:1, 5:1, 2:1, 1:1, 0:1 and MeOH) to
afford several fractions. Eight main fractions were obtained
which was confirmed by TLC. Every fraction was dissolved
in methanol and centrifuged at 12,000 rpm for 10 min. The
supernatant was collected and subjected to preparative HPLC

Antibacterial Activity Fungi Screening Method
The technique designed in this article showed a new approach
to screen antibacterial agents. Manipulation of the 96-well
plate with eight cut wells allowed access of culturing eight
different fungal strains and testing its antibacterial activities
simultaneously. Eight wells of the 96-well plate were cut at the
bottom using a knife, and after antisepsis, the plate was placed
in an enlarged specified petri dish containing agar medium
(Fig. 1a). The agar medium consisted of MH broth and PDA
medium (6 g potato extract, 20 g glucose, 5 g NaCl, 2 g beef
extract powder, 17.5 g casamino acids, 1.5 g soluble starch,
20 g agar per liter distilled water) which is suitable for fungal
and bacterial growth. The eight manipulated wells allowed the
penetration of the agar medium through the opened holes (Fig.
1b). After solidification of the medium, 20 μL mother liquor
of the eight different fungi strains, namely, Aspergillus sp. XZ1, Aspergillus versicolor XZ-2, Aspergillus sp. YQ-1,
Penicillium sp. YQ-5, Penicillium sp. Y-5-1, Penicillium sp.
Y-5-2, Aspergillus versicolor XZ-4, and Acremonium sp. HS1, were inoculated in the eight manipulated wells respectively
(Fig. 1c). The enlarged specified petri dish containing the 96well plate was covered with a lid and was left for incubation
for 1 week. During growth of the different strains, metabolites
were secreted and permeated in the agar medium which was
then referred to as Bconditioned agar^ (Fig. 1d). After 1 week,
the plate with the conditioned agar was inverted, and using the
spread plate technique, Bacillus subtilis, Staphylococcus
aureus, and Escherichia coli were inoculated at the back of
the medium (Fig. 1e) and visible inhibition zones were monitored as antibacterial activity after 20 h at 37 °C.

Due to the considerable antibacterial activity against three
representative test strains (E. coli, S. aureus, B. subtilis),
Penicillium sp. Y-5-2 was selected to be cultivated in large
scale for isolation of potent antibacterial compounds. From
the large-scale fermentation of Penicillium sp. Y-5-2, five novel metabolites (4, 9, 11, 12, and 13), two known isocoumarins
aspergillumarins A (8) and B (10) (Li et al. 2012; Miao et al.
2012), and six known austin derivatives dehydroaustin (1)
(Hayashi et al. 1994), dehydroaustinol (2) (Hayashi et al.
1994), 7-hydroxydehydroaustin (3) (Arunpanichlert et al.
2015), austin (5) (Holker et al. 1983; Lo et al. 2012), austinol
(6) (Holker et al. 1983; Lo et al. 2012), austinolide (7) (Lo
et al. 2012; Fill et al. 2007), and a phenol antibiotic,
pestalotionol (14) (Arunpanichlert et al. 2015), were isolated
from fungus. The isolation, structure elucidation, and biological activities of the isolated metabolites are discussed below.
Compound 4 was isolated as a white, amorphous powder
with the molecular formula assigned as C32H40O13 on the
basis of its HRESIMS data. The UV spectrum showed an
absorption band at λmax 215 nm, indicating the existence of
lactone. The IR spectrum exhibited absorption bands at
3503 cm−1 (hydroxyl group), 1773 cm−1 (γ-lactone carbonyl
group), 1750 cm−1 (δ-lactone carbonyl group), 1713 cm−1
(ketone carbonyl group), and 1648 cm−1 (α,β-unsaturated ester carbonyl group). Detailed inspection of the 1H NMR and
13
C NMR data (Table 1) revealed that compound 4 belongs to
the austin class. Analysis of the 1H and 13C NMR data
(Table 1) indicated that the structure of 4 is similar to the
known compound 3, 7-hydroxydehydroaustin
(Arunpanichlert et al. 2015). The differences lay in three features. One is the presence of prenyl group [δH 6.76, (m, 1H),

The antibacterial activities of all isolated compounds
were evaluated with three representative bacteria, E. coli,
S. aureus, and B. subtilis (Table 3). Compound 14 showed
potent activity against S. aureus and B. subtilis, with MIC
values of 8 and 2 μg/mL, respectively. Compounds 9, 11,
and 13 exhibited weak activity against E. coli, with MIC
values of around 32 μg/mL for each. Compounds 8 and 10
were previously reported to exhibit weak antibacterial activity against S. aureus and B. subtilis at a concentration of
50 μg/mL (Li et al. 2012). All isolated compounds were
also evaluated for their cytotoxic activity against human
hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) cell lines (HepG2,
SMMC-7721, and Bel-7402), but none of them exhibited
cytotoxic activity.

Conclusion
This article provides an express technique of Bpin pointing^
typical strains possessing the ability of generating antibiotics
against bacteria. In this study, Penicillium sp. Y-5-2 selected
out by the new method showed potent antibacterial activities
against three representative test strains and three of the new
isolated metabolites exhibited antibiotic activity against
E. coli with MIC value around 32 μg/mL. Compound 14
exhibited strong antibacterial activities against S. aureus and
B. subtilis, proving that the Bconditional agar^ technique is an
efficient and less time-consuming way to select potentially
bioactive antibiotics producing microorganism strains. The
hydrothermal vent sediment-derived fungus Penicillium sp.
Y-5-2 also displayed a broad spectrum of chemical diversity
of secondary metabolites with a new isocoumarin skeleton
and different classes of natural compounds.
