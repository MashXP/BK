# Chapter 1 & 2: Introduction & Basic Concepts

## 1. Concepts
**Fermentation (Bio-chemical):** Energy generation for cells where molecules like glucose are **broken down anaerobically** (e.g., Lactic acid, Alcohol fermentation).

**Fermentation (Broad):** **Chemical breakdown** of a substance by bacteria, yeasts, or other microorganisms, with or without oxygen.

**Fermentation Technology:** 
- The **application of fermentation** for the production of compounds 
- useful in energy, material, pharmaceutical, chemical, and food industries.
 `Fermentation Technology = Fermentation + Technology (Engineering).`

## 2. Range of Fermentation Processes
There are **4** major groups of commercially important fermentations:
### 2.1 Microbial Biomass
1.  **Yeast Production:** Baking, beverage (beer, wine), bioethanol, pharmacy.
2.  **Single-Cell Protein (SCP):** Edible unicellular microorganisms.

> [!success] **Advantages of SCP**
> - **Fast growth:** Microorganisms grow much faster than protein-rich grains.
> - **High quality:** Protein content and quality are superior (60-80%).
> - **Inexpensive:** Uses a wide range of cheap raw materials.
> - **Simple process:** The production process is easy and efficient.
> - **Year-round:** Can be produced regardless of season.
> - **Efficient:** Cost-effective and energy-efficient.

> [!failure] **Disadvantages of SCP**
> - **Quality Control:** Harvesting and purification are difficult.
> - **Incomplete Nutrition:** Not a suitable source for all essential amino acids (used as a supplement).

> [!info] **Required Properties for SCP Microorganisms**
> 1. Absence of pathogenicity and toxicity.
> 2. High protein content and quality.
> 3. Good digestibility and organoleptic qualities.
> 4. High growth rate.
> 5. Adaptability to unusual conditions (pH, temp, minerals).
> 6. Ability to utilize carbon and nitrogen sources.
### 2.2 Microbial Enzymes

> [!success] **Advantages**
> - Able to be produced in large quantities by established techniques.
> - Infinitely easier to improve productivity than plant or animal systems.
> - Recombinant DNA technology allows synthesis of animal enzymes in microbes.

> [!info] **Applications** 
> Baking, brewing, cereals, chocolate, coffee, confectionery, dairy, etc.
### 2.3 Microbial Metabolites

> [!info] **Primary Metabolites**
> *   **Production Phase:** **Log phase** (during active growth).
> *   **Function:** Essential for growth, development, and reproduction.
> *   **Examples:** Amino acids, nucleotides, ethanol, organic acids, vitamins.

> [!warning] **Secondary Metabolites**
> *   **Production Phase:** **Stationary phase** (end of growth).
> *   **Function:** 
> 	* Not essential for growth; 
> 	* often have ecological functions (e.g., defense).
> 	* Only produce low amount, controlled by induction, quorum sensing, growth rate, stress...
> *   **Examples:** Antibiotics (e.g., Penicillin), toxins, pigments.

> [!tip] **Overproduction Strategy**
> *   **Goal:** Enhance productivity beyond the organism's natural requirements.
> *   **Approach:** 
>     *   **Metabolic Engineering:** Modifying pathways using genomics/proteomics.
>     *   **Process Optimization:** Adjusting cultural conditions.
### 2.4 Recombinant Products
Genes from higher organisms may be introduced into microbial cells such that the recipients are capable of synthesizing 'foreign' (or heterologous) proteins.

## 3. History of Development

| Era | Main Products | Key Technology & Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pre-1900** | Alcohol (Beer, Wine), Vinegar, Food (Cheese, Yoghurt, Soy sauce) | **Batch**. Wooden barrels. Thermometers/hydrometers. Pure yeast cultures introduced (1886). Pasteur establishes microbiology. |
| **1900-1940** | Bakers' yeast, Glycerol, Citric acid, Lactic acid, Acetone/Butanol | **Batch & Fed-batch**. Steel vessels. Air spargers & mechanical stirring. Off-line pH control. |
| **1940-Date** | Antibiotics (Penicillin), Amino acids, Enzymes | **Aseptic Aerated Fermenters**. Sterilizable electrodes (pH, O2). Computerized control loops. Mutation/Selection programs. |
| **1964-Date** | Single-Cell Protein (SCP) | **Continuous Culture**. Pressure cycle/jet vessels. Genetic engineering of producer strains. |
| **1982-Date** | Heterologous proteins, Monoclonal antibodies | **Animal Cell Reactors**. Introduction of foreign genes into microbial/animal hosts. |
| **2000-Date** | Synthetic Biology applications, Bulk chemicals | **Synthetic Biology**. Improvement of established fermentations. |

## 4. Basic Component Parts of a Fermentation Process
1.  **Media Formulation:** For inoculum development and production.
2.  **Sterilization:** Of medium, fermenters, and ancillary equipment.
3.  **Inoculum:** Production of active, pure culture in sufficient quantity.
4.  **Growth:** Maintaining optimum conditions for product formation.
5.  **Extraction:** Purification and downstream processing.
6.  **Disposal:** Handling of effluents produced by the process.

## 5. Fermentation Methods

#### Classification Criteria
- **Medium:** Liquid (SmF) vs. Solid State (SSF).
- **Oxygen:** Aerobic vs. Anaerobic.
- **Aeration:** With vs. Without stirring/aeration.
- **Control:** Controlled (pH, temp) vs. Natural.
- **Process Type:** Batch, Fed-batch, Continuous.

#### A. Batch Culture
> [!info] **Definition**
> - ***Closed** system
> - initial, **limited** nutrients. 
> - **No addition** typically (except gases/pH control).*

- **Phases:** Lag -> Exponential (Log) -> Deceleration -> Stationary -> Death.

> [!success] **Advantages**
> - Reduced risk of contamination or cell mutation.
> - Lower capital investment.

> [!failure] **Limitations**
> - Constantly changing environment (nutrient consumption/waste buildup).
> - Low productivity due to high downtime (cleaning/sterilizing between batches).
> - Toxic metabolite buildup can inhibit growth.

#### B. Fed-Batch Culture
> [!info] **Definition**
> - **Nutrients are added** aseptically.
> - **Volume** of liquid culture increases.*

> [!success] **Advantages**
> - Long-term synthesis of products.
> - Increased yield and productivity.
> - Broth viscosity and toxin concentrations can be reduced.

> [!failure] **Limitations**
> - Complex process to conduct.
> - Requires high technical skills for operation and maintenance.
> - Requires sophisticated equipment to control feed flow.

#### C. Continuous Culture
> [!info] **Definition**
> - **Nutrients** continually added.
> - **Culture removed** at the same time.
> - **Constant volume** and steady-state established.*

> [!success] **Advantages**
> - Optimum conditions can be set for maximum long-term synthesis.
> - Higher productivity per unit volume due to greatly reduced downtime.

> [!failure] **Limitations**
> - Sterility maintenance can be tricky.
> - Increased risk of contamination and/or genetic changes.
> - Requires sophisticated equipment to control the flows.
