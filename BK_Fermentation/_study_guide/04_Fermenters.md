# Chapter 4: Fermenter

## 1. Introduction
The development of large-scale aseptic fermentation vessels began during the First World War (1914–1918). 
**Chaim Weizmann** in Great Britain developed a deep liquid fermentation process using ***Clostridium acetobutylicum*** for the production of **acetone**, which was essential for war efforts.

---

## 2. Aseptic Operation and Containment

> [!info] **Definitions**
> - **Aseptic Operation:** Involves protection against contamination by unwanted microorganisms.
> - **Containment:** Involves prevention of escape of viable cells from a fermenter or downstream equipment into the environment.

### 2.1 Criteria to Assess Risk
*   The known pathogenicity of the microorganism.
*   The virulence or level of pathogenicity.
*   The number of organisms required to initiate an infection.
*   The routes of infection.
*   The volumes of organisms used in the fermentation process.
*   Ease of prophylaxis (prevention) and treatment.

### 2.2 WHO Risk Groups
The World Health Organization (WHO) classifies infectious microorganisms into four risk groups:

| Risk Group | Risk Level | Description | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **RG 1** | Minimal | Microorganisms that usually do not cause human diseases. | *E. coli* K12, *Bacillus subtilis* |
| **RG 2** | Moderate | Microorganisms that cause treatable or self-healing diseases. | *Staphylococcus aureus*, HBV, *E. coli* O157 |
| **RG 3** | High | Highly contagious microorganisms that cause serious diseases. | *M. tuberculosis*, *Brucella* sp., West Nile Virus |
| **RG 4** | Very High | Highly contagious, cause serious diseases/epidemics, high mortality. | Ebola, Marburg, CCHFV |

### 2.3 Regulatory Standards
- **GLP (Good Laboratory Practice):** Quality system for organizational processes and conditions under which non-clinical studies are planned, performed, and recorded.
- **GMP (Good Manufacturing Practice):** Ensures products meet quality standards. (The 5 P's: People, Products, Processes, Procedures, Premises).
- **GILSP (Good Industrial Large Scale Practice):** Guidelines for quality, safety, and efficiency in large-scale industrial processes.

## 3. Basic Functions & Requirements of a Fermenter

1.  **Aseptic Operation:** Capable of being operated aseptically for a number of days and meet containment regulations.
2.  **Aeration & Agitation:** Provide adequate oxygen/mixing without damaging organisms or causing excessive foam.
3.  **Efficiency:** Power consumption should be as low as possible.
4.  **Control System:** Precise control of temperature, pH, dissolved oxygen (DO), etc.
5.  **Sampling:** Facilities for aseptic sampling must be provided.
6.  **Evaporation:** Evaporation losses should not be excessive.
7.  **Labor:** Designed for minimal labor in operation, harvesting, cleaning, and maintenance.
8.  **Surface:** Smooth internal surfaces to prevent microbial attachment and facilitate cleaning.
9.  **Materials:** Use the cheapest materials that enable satisfactory results.

## 5. Fermenter Materials

> [!tip] **Requirements**
> Materials must withstand **pressure sterilization (*in situ*)**, resist **corrosion**, be **non-toxic**, and be **cost-effective**.

-   **Small Scale:** Glass or stainless steel. Glass allows for easy examination of the interior and has smooth, non-toxic surfaces.
-   **Pilot/Industrial Scale:** Normally constructed of **stainless steel**.

---

## 6. Agitation and Aeration

### 6.1 Agitation
**Goal:** Ensure uniform suspension of cells in a homogeneous nutrient medium and maintain uniform temperature.
- **Factors:** Vessel parameters (diameter/volume), mixing capacity (motor power), impeller type, and medium viscosity.
- **Types of Impellers:**
    - (a) Disc turbine
    - (b) Vaned disc
    - (c) Open turbine (variable pitch)
    - (d) Marine propeller

### 6.2 Aeration
**Goal:** Provide oxygen (for aerobic fermentation), mix the culture, and release exhaust gases.
- **Inlet Air:** Must be filtered.
- **Source:** Air compressor.
- **Sparger:** Device for introducing air into the liquid.
    - **Porous sparger** (tấm phun lỗ)
    - **Orifice nozzle sparger** (vòi phun lỗ)

---

## 7. Ancillary Equipment & Sensors
- **Boiler (Lò hơi):** Used for heating and sterilization. Uses fuels like DO, FO, LPG, or biomass.
- **Cooling Tower (Thiết bị giải nhiệt):** Three types: **Jacket** (dạng áo), **Coil** (dạng ống xoắn), and **Tube sheet** (dạng bó ống).
- **Cyclone:** Used in industrial-scale fermenters to handle foam and outlet air.
- **Sensors:** Monitor **pH**, **temperature**, and **Dissolved Oxygen (DO2)**.

---

## 8. Types of Fermenters

### 8.1 Stirred Tank Fermenter (Hệ lên men thùng khuấy)
Most common type used in industry for both aerobic and anaerobic fermentation.
- **Geometry:** Height-to-diameter ratio of 2:1 to 3:1.
- **Impeller:** Diameter is typically 0.3–0.4 of the vessel diameter.

> [!success] **Advantages**
> - Very effective mixing.
> - Versatile (aerobic/anaerobic).

> [!failure] **Disadvantages**
> - High energy consumption.
> - High investment cost.
> - High **shear force** (can damage sensitive cells like mammalian cells).

### 8.2 Airlift Fermenter (Hệ lên men vòng lực đẩy không khí)
Uses air to circulate the liquid through a **riser** (rich bubbles) and **downcomer** (poor bubbles).
- **Types:** External-loop and Internal-loop vessels.

> [!success] **Advantages**
> - Simple design.
> - Low shear stress.

> [!failure] **Disadvantages**
> - Potential "dead zones".
> - High foam production.

### 8.3 Tower / Bubble Column Fermenter (Cột tháp / Cột bong bóng)
Aeration and agitation are achieved solely by gas sparging.
- **Geometry:** High height-to-diameter ratio (6:1 to 10:1).
- **History:** First described for citric acid production.

> [!success] **Advantages**
> - Simple, low investment, and easy maintenance.

> [!failure] **Disadvantages**
> - Low mixing efficiency.

### 8.4 Photobioreactor (Hệ thống quang sinh học)
Designed for photosynthetic organisms (e.g., microalgae) using transparent materials.
- **Configurations:** Vertical column, Horizontal column, Flat plate.

> [!success] **Advantages**
> - Save area and high efficiency.
> - Precise control and minimized contamination compared to open ponds.

> [!failure] **Disadvantages**
> - High construction and operation costs.
