**Industrial microorganisms** are microorganisms to produce **food** or **industrial products** in ***large*** quantities. include:
- naturally occurring organisms
- laboratory selected mutants
- or even genetically modified organisms (GMOs)

### 1.1 Applications of Industrial Microorganisms
*   **Biopharmaceuticals:**
    *   Vaccines, hormones, enzymes, monoclonal antibodies.
    *   Used for treatment of diseases (infectious, cancer, diabetes).
*   **Biofuels and Bioenergy:**
    *   Synthesis of biogas, biofuels, biodiesel from renewable biomass.
*   **Food and Beverage:**
    *   Production of enzymes, probiotics, plant-based proteins.
*   **Agriculture:**
    *   Genetically engineered crops (e.g., Bt corn, Bt cotton).
    *   Express bacterial toxins (e.g., from *Bacillus thuringiensis*) to eliminate pests.
*   **Environmental Management:**
    *   Tackling pollution and waste management.

### 2.2 Challenges of Industrial Microorganisms
*   **Development of GMO strains:**
    *   Requires extensive genetic engineering and metabolic pathway optimization (complex and time-consuming).
    *   Challenges in maintaining strain stability under industrial conditions.
*   **Waste Management:**
    *   Spent microbial cultures and fermentation residues must be treated to avoid adverse environmental effects (increases costs).
*   **Regulation:**
    *   Strict guidelines from regulatory agencies to ensure bioproduct safety.

## 2. Isolation of Industrial Microorganisms

### 2.1 Criteria in the Choice of Microorganisms
1.  **Nutritional Characteristics:** The nutritional requirements of the organism.
2.  **Optimum Temperature:** The temperature range for optimal growth.
3.  **Process Suitability:** Reaction with equipment and suitability to the process type.
4.  **Stability:** Genetic stability and amenability to genetic manipulation.
5.  **Productivity:** High yield of product per unit time.
6.  **Recovery:** Ease of product recovery from the culture.

### 2.2 Isolation Methods

#### A. Selective Force (Pressure)
*   **Definition:** Using specific conditions to isolate organisms that can survive/grow while others cannot.
*   **Examples:**
    *   **Thermophiles:** Heat-loving.
    *   **Psychrotrophs:** Cold-tolerant.
    *   **Acidophiles:** Acid-loving.
    *   **Halophiles:** Salt-loving.

#### B. Enrichment Liquid Culture
*   **Method:**
    *   Frequently carried out in shake flasks.
    *   Inoculate enriched culture into fresh medium.
    *   Repeated subculturing (several times) to isolate the dominant organism.
    *   Spread small inoculum onto solidified medium.
*   **Result:** The dominant organism will be the fastest growing of those capable of growth.

#### C. Enrichment Cultures using Solidified Media
*   **Application:** Isolation of certain enzyme or active-compound producers.
*   **Method:** Usually involves the use of a **selective medium**.
*   **Examples:** Protease producing bacteria (clear zones on plates), Antimicrobial activity (inhibition zones).

#### D. Simple Liquid Culture
*   **Goal:** Isolate the organism with the **highest affinity** for the limiting substrate.
*   **Considerations:** Temperature, pH, and other environmental factors.

### 2.3 Improvement of Industrial Microorganisms
*   **Limitation:** Optimizing culture medium and growth conditions is limited by the organism's maximum ability.
*   **Solution:** Modify the genome to increase potential yield.
*   **Methods:**
    *   Selecting natural variants.
    *   Selecting induced mutants.
    *   Selecting recombinants.

## 3. Culture Preservation

### 3.1 Roles and Requirements
*   **Goal:** Retain the desirable characteristics that led to the organism's selection.
*   **Requirements:**
    1.  Eliminate genetic change.
    2.  Protect against contamination.
    3.  Retain viability.
*   **General Methods:** Storing at reduced temperature or in a dehydrated form.

### 3.2 Storage at Reduced Temperature

#### A. Storage in Liquid Culture or on Petri Dish
*   **Conditions:** Refrigerator at 4°C.
*   **Duration:** Short period (strains used daily or weekly).
*   **Protocol:** Plates need to be sealed with **Parafilm**.
    *   *Parafilm benefits:* Prevents contamination, seals various shapes, permeable to gases (O2, CO2, water vapor) allowing off-gassing without compromising integrity.

#### B. Storage on Agar Slopes
*   **Method:** Microbial cultures grown on agar slopes (slants).
*   **Conditions:** Refrigerator at 4°C.
*   **Subculturing:** Approximately **6-month intervals**.
*   **Stock Culture:**
    *   *Definition:* A culture used to maintain viability for future reference.
    *   *Importance:* Maintains viability and purity (free from contamination).
    *   *Preparation:* Pure Culture -> Agar Medium -> Culture Tubes -> Autoclave -> Cotton Plug/Foil -> Inoculate -> Incubate.
*   **Pros & Cons:**
    *   **Advantages:** Simple, does not require much facilities.
    *   **Disadvantages:** Risk of contamination, loss of viability, genetic mutation.

### 3.3 Storage at Freezing Temperature
*   **Principle:** The colder the storage temperature, the longer the culture will retain viable cells.
*   **Cryopreservation:**
    *   Using very low temperatures to prevent cell death and induce a **hibernation state**.
*   **Advantages:**
    *   Reduced risk of microbial contamination.
    *   Reduced risk of cross-contamination with other cell lines.
    *   Reduced risk of genetic drift and morphological changes.
    *   Reduced costs of consumables and staff time.

#### A. Cryodamage
*   **Cause:** Ice crystal formation and dehydration.
*   **Effects:**
    *   Rupture of membranes.
    *   High concentration of salt (solutes concentration increases as water freezes).
*   **Process:** Water is lost from the cell -> Solvents around cell freeze -> Solutes concentrate.

#### B. Cryoprotectants
*   **Function:** Prevent cells from dying during freezing.
*   **Example:** **Glycerol**.
    *   *Mechanism:* Depresses the freezing point of cells, enhances supercooling, and protects from ice.
    *   *Natural Occurrence:* Produced by many fish and insects.
    *   *Usage:* Added to microorganisms at a final concentration of **~10-15%**.

#### C. Freezer Categories

**1. Laboratory Freezers**
*   **Temperature:** -20°C to -40°C.
*   **Duration:** Moderate periods (e.g., 1 year).
*   **Pros:** Inexpensive, readily available.
*   **Cons:** Not cold enough for long-term storage.

**2. Ultralow Freezers**
*   **Temperature:** Around -86°C.
*   **Duration:** Several years.
*   **Pros:** Good for medium-long term.
*   **Cons:** Expensive to buy/maintain. Molecular motion still occurs, leading to viability decline over time (requires monitoring).

**3. Cryogenic Freezers**
*   **Temperature:** Below -130°C (often -140°C in liquid nitrogen vapor).
*   **Method:** Liquid nitrogen or specialized mechanical systems.
*   **Duration:** Many years (e.g., 15 years) without significant loss.
*   **Pros:** Most effective for long-term storage. Easiest method compared to freeze-drying.
*   **Cons:** Cost, vulnerability to power outages/mechanical failures/liquid nitrogen supply issues.

### 3.4 Storage by Freeze Drying (Lyophilization)
*   **Definition:** Freezing of a culture followed by drying under vacuum, resulting in the **sublimation** of cell water.
*   **Process:**
    1.  Culture grown to maximum stationary phase.
    2.  Resuspended in protective medium (milk, serum, sodium glutamate).
    3.  Transferred to ampoule, frozen, and subjected to high vacuum.
    4.  Ampoule is sealed after sublimation.
*   **Storage:** Refrigerator.
*   **Duration:** Viable for **10 years or more**.
*   **Applicability:** Suitable for microorganisms, but **NOT** for animal cell cultures.
*   **Pros & Cons:**
    *   **Advantages:**
        *   Once dried, needs no further attention.
        *   Storage equipment (refrigerator) is cheap and reliable.
        *   Considered second only to liquid nitrogen storage in effectiveness.
    *   **Disadvantages:**
        *   Tedious to open and revitalize.
        *   Several sub-cultures may be needed before cells regain typical characteristics.

### 3.5 Quality Control of Preserved Stock Cultures
*   **Key Criteria:**
    1.  **Redundancy:** Cultures should be preserved by at least **two different methods**.
    2.  **Generations:** The number of transfers/generations before preservation should be kept to a minimum to reduce the risk of degeneration.
    3.  **Master vs. Working Stocks:**
        *   A bank of identical **Master Stock Cultures** must be established as the definitive source.
        *   A bank of **Working Stock Cultures** is sourced from a master culture.
        *   The master culture bank should contain sufficient ampoules to minimize re-generation of master stocks.
    4.  **Inoculum:** Each fermentation run should be provided by an ampoule from the bank of working stock cultures.
    5.  **Evaluation:**
        *   The original culture, master, and working preserved stocks should all be evaluated to ensure their quality.
        *   **Assessment parameters:** Purity, fermentation performance, and variability of the culture.
