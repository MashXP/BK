# Molecular Biology Comprehensive Study Guide

## Chapter 1: Macrobiomolecules

### 1. Overview
The four main classes of macrobiomolecules are:
*   **Nucleic Acids**: DNA and RNA
*   **Proteins**: Functional and structural roles
*   **Lipids**: Membrane structure and signaling
*   **Carbohydrates**: Energy and recognition

### 2. Nucleic Acids (DNA & RNA)
*   **Composition**: 
    *   **DNA**: Deoxyribonucleic acid. Bases: A, T, G, C. Double helix (34 Angstrom/10 bases twist). Phosphate outside. Stable.
    *   **RNA**: Ribonucleic acid. Bases: A, U, G, C. Single-stranded (mostly). Unstable (UV sensible, degrades easily).
*   **Structures**:
    *   **Secondary**: Hairpin, Stem-loop.
    *   **Tertiary**: Complex folding (e.g., tRNA).
*   **Types of RNA**:
    *   Messenger RNA (mRNA)
    *   Transfer RNA (tRNA)
    *   Ribosomal RNA (rRNA)
    *   Small nuclear RNA (snRNA)
    *   MicroRNA (miRNA)
    *   Small interfering RNA (siRNA)
*   **Analysis**:
    *   **Electrophoresis**: Separation by size/charge.
    *   **Crystallography**: X-ray diffraction for structure (Double Helix).

### 3. Proteins
*   **Interactions**: Hydrogen bonds, Peptide bonds, Disulfide (S-S) bridges, Ionic bonds, Van Der Waals forces.
*   **Functions**: Signaling, Structure, Catalysis (Enzymes), Regulation ("on/off"), Movement, Transport.
*   **Analysis Methods**:
    *   **Chromatography**: Separation based on size, charge, or affinity.
    *   **Western Blotting**: Detection of specific proteins using antibodies.
    *   **MALDI-TOF**: Mass spectrometry for identification.

### 4. Lipids
*   **Structure**: Hydrophilic heads, Hydrophobic tails (Phospholipid bilayer).
*   **Lipid Rafts**: Specialized membrane microdomains for signaling.
*   **Classification**: Saturated vs. Unsaturated. LIPID MAPS categories (8 categories).
*   **Functions**: Structure (membrane), Metabolism, Signaling.

### 5. Carbohydrates
*   **Functions**: Structure, Metabolism, Signaling.
*   **Analysis**: Chromatography, Electrophoresis (borate treatment).
*   **Glycoproteins/Glycolipids**: Cell recognition and signaling.

## Chapter 2: Basic Molecular Genetic Mechanism

### 1. DNA Stability and Flexibility
*   **Flexibility**: Allows for Denaturation, Mutation, Replication, Recombination, Crossing-over, and Mobile elements.
*   **Stability**: Maintained by Renaturation, Repair mechanisms, Semi-constitutive replication, and the Double helix structure.
*   **Denaturation vs. Renaturation**: DNA melting curve (Tm) depends on G-C content (higher G-C = higher Tm).

### 2. DNA Replication
*   **Mechanism**:
    1.  **Unwinding**: Helicase (ATP-dependent) separates strands.
    2.  **Priming**: Primase adds RNA primers.
    3.  **Elongation**: DNA Polymerase adds nucleotides (5' to 3').
        *   **Leading Strand**: Continuous synthesis.
        *   **Lagging Strand**: Discontinuous (Okazaki fragments).
    4.  **Ligation**: DNA Ligase joins fragments.
*   **Key Enzymes**:
    *   **Helicase (e.g., DnaB, RecBCD)**: Unwinds DNA. RecBCD has RecB (3'-5' translocase) and RecD (5'-3' translocase).
    *   **Topoisomerase**: Relieves supercoiling.
    *   **SSB (Single-strand binding proteins)**: Stabilize unwound DNA.
*   **Prokaryote vs. Eukaryote**:
    *   **Origin (Ori)**: Single (Prokaryote) vs. Multiple (Eukaryote).
    *   **Shape**: Circular (Prokaryote) vs. Linear (Eukaryote).
    *   **Polymerases**: 1 main type (Prokaryote) vs. 3 types ($\alpha, \delta, \epsilon$ in Eukaryotes).
    *   **Primase**: Free (Prokaryote) vs. Associated with Pol $\alpha$ (Eukaryote).

### 3. DNA Repair Mechanisms
*   **Base Excision Repair (BER)**:
    *   Removes damaged bases (e.g., via DNA glycosylase).
    *   AP endonuclease cuts backbone.
    *   DNA Pol $\beta$ and Ligase seal the gap.
*   **Double Strand Break Repair**:
    *   **Homologous Recombination**: Uses sister chromatid as template (error-free).
    *   **Non-homologous End Joining (NHEJ)**: Ligate broken ends (error-prone).

### 4. Mobile Elements (Transposons)
*   **Mechanisms**:
    *   **Class 1 (Retrotransposons)**: "Copy and Paste" (DNA -> RNA -> DNA -> Insert). Uses Reverse Transcriptase.
    *   **Class 2 (DNA Transposons)**: "Cut and Paste" (Excise -> Insert). Uses Transposase.
*   **Impacts**:
    *   **Advantageous**: New regulatory networks, evolutionary adaptation/diversity.
    *   **Deleterious**: Lethal insertions, mutational meltdown, somatic diseases (cancer).
*   **Mammalian Protection**:
    *   KRAB/KAP1 histone modification (methylation).
    *   APOBEC sequence editing (C to U).
    *   PIWI proteins/piRNA (cleavage and methylation).

## Chapter 3: Transcription and Regulation

### 1. Gene Structure
*   **Components**: Promoter, RNA coding sequence, Terminator.
*   **Promoter Regions**:
    *   **Prokaryote**: -35 (TTGACA) and -10 (TATAAT/Pribnow box).
    *   **Eukaryote**: TATA box (at -30).

### 2. Transcription Mechanism
*   **Prokaryote**:
    *   **Initiation**: Sigma ($\sigma$) factor binds promoter (-35, -10), recruits RNA Polymerase holoenzyme.
    *   **Elongation**: Transcription bubble moves.
    *   **Termination**:
        *   **Rho-independent**: Stem-loop structure forms (C-G rich), followed by U-rich tail.
        *   **Rho-dependent**: Rho protein (helicase) binds C-rich region, catches up to polymerase, unwinds hybrid.
*   **Eukaryote**:
    *   **Initiation**: Transcription factors (TFIID/TBP) bind TATA box, recruit RNA Pol II (Pre-initiation complex).
    *   **Elongation**: FACT complex removes histones. C-terminal domain (CTD) phosphorylation.
    *   **Termination**: Poly-A signal (AAUAAA) triggers cleavage and polyadenylation.
    *   **RNA Polymerases**:
        *   **Pol I**: rRNA (except 5S).
        *   **Pol II**: mRNA, miRNA, lncRNA.
        *   **Pol III**: 5S rRNA, tRNA, snRNA.

### 3. Gene Regulation
*   **Prokaryotic Operons**:
    *   **Lac Operon** (Inducible):
        *   **Repression**: No lactose -> Repressor binds Operator -> No transcription.
        *   **Induction**: Lactose present -> Binds repressor -> Repressor releases -> Transcription.
        *   **Activation**: Low Glucose -> High cAMP -> cAMP-CAP complex binds promoter -> Boosts transcription.
    *   **Trp Operon** (Repressible):
        *   High Tryptophan -> Trp binds Repressor -> Repressor binds Operator -> Block transcription.
*   **Eukaryotic Regulation**:
    *   **Transcriptional**: Enhancers/Silencers (distal elements), Mediators, Activators/Repressors.
    *   **Post-Transcriptional**:
        *   **5' Capping**: 7-methylguanosine cap (stability, translation).
        *   **Splicing**: Spliceosome removes introns, joins exons.
        *   **3' Polyadenylation**: Poly-A tail (stability).
        *   **miRNA/siRNA**:
            *   **miRNA**: Imperfect match -> Translational repression.
            *   **siRNA**: Perfect match -> mRNA degradation (RISC complex).

## Chapter 4: Epigenetics

### 1. DNA Methylation (Eukaryote)
*   **Mechanism**: Addition of methyl group to Cytosine/Adenine.
*   **Context**: Occurs at CpG regions (G following C). 90-98% of CpG sites are methylated in mammals.
*   **Enzymes (DNMTs)**:
    *   **DNMT1**: Maintains methylation (mother to daughter cells).
    *   **DNMT3a/3b**: De novo methylation.
    *   **DNMT3L**: Supports DNMT3a/3b, inhibits transposons.

### 2. Histone Modification
*   **Nucleosome**: 147 bp DNA wrapped 1.67 times around histone octamer (2x H2A, H2B, H3, H4).
*   **Effect**: Modifications on H3/H4 tails affect DNA-histone interaction and gene expression (open vs. closed chromatin).

### 3. Chromatin Structure
*   **Heterochromatin**: Condensed, transcriptionally repressed.
*   **Euchromatin**: Open/relaxed, transcriptionally active.
*   **3D Folding**: Brings regulatory factors closer to target genes.

### 4. Non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs)
*   **Short**: miRNA, siRNA, piRNA.
*   **Long**: lncRNA (e.g., acts as guide, scaffold/architect, or enhancer).

### 5. Prokaryotic Epigenetics
*   **R-M System**: Restriction-Modification system (REase + MTase) to protect against foreign DNA (phage).
*   **Dam/Dcm Methylation**:
    *   **Dam Methylase**: Methylates Adenine (GATC sequence). Regulates replication (Ori), mismatch repair (MutS/L), and gene expression.
    *   **Orphan MTases**: Operate independently (e.g., CcrM cell cycle regulator).

## Chapter 5: Translation

### 1. Comparison
*   **Prokaryote (70S)**: 50S (5S, 23S rRNA) + 30S (16S rRNA). Shine-Dalgarno sequence. fMet initiator. Simultaneous transcription/translation.
*   **Eukaryote (80S)**: 60S (5S, 5.8S, 28S rRNA) + 40S (18S rRNA). 5' Cap dependent. Met initiator. Separated processes.

### 2. Mechanism (Prokaryote)
*   **Initiation**: 30S subunit binds IFs and mRNA (Shine-Dalgarno). fMet-tRNA binds. 50S joins.
*   **Elongation**:
    *   Aminoacyl-tRNA enters A site.
    *   Peptidyl transferase forms peptide bond.
    *   Translocation (ribosome moves).
*   **Termination**: Release factors recognize Stop codon (UAA, UAG, UGA). Polypeptide released.

### 3. Mechanism (Eukaryote)
*   **Initiation**:
    *   **Cap-dependent**: eIFs bind 5' Cap, recruit 40S subunit. Scans for AUG.
    *   **Cap-independent**: IRES (Internal Ribosome Entry Site).
*   **Elongation**: Similar to prokaryotes (eEFs).
*   **Termination**: eRF1, eRF3.

## Chapter 6: Protein

### 1. Protein Sorting and Transport
*   **Secretory Pathway**: Cytosol -> ER (via Signal Sequence) -> Golgi -> Lysosome/Plasma Membrane/Secreted.
*   **Mitochondrial Transport**: TIM/TOM complexes transport proteins across inner/outer membranes.
*   **Nuclear Transport**: Nuclear pores.

### 2. Post-Translational Modification (PTM)
*   **Glycosylation**: Adding sugar (ER, Golgi). Stabilizes structure.
    *   **N-linked**: Asn residue (ER).
*   **Disulfide Bonds (S-S)**:
    *   Formed in ER.
    *   Enzyme: **PDI** (Protein Disulfide Isomerase). Stabilizes tertiary structure.
*   **Folding**:
    *   **Chaperones (e.g., BiP)**: Bind newly synthesized proteins to prevent aggregation and assist folding.

### 3. Protein Degradation
*   **Ubiquitin-Proteasome System**:
    1.  **Ubiquitination**: E1/E2/E3 enzymes add ubiquitin chain (Lysine residue) to target protein.
    2.  **Degradation**: 26S Proteasome recognizes ubiquitin, unfolds, and chops protein into peptides.
*   **ERAD (ER-Associated Degradation)**: Misfolded proteins in ER are retro-translocated to cytosol for degradation.

### 4. TOR Pathway (mTOR)
*   **Function**: Central regulator of growth and metabolism.
*   **mTORC1**: Kinase complex. Activated by Rheb-GTP.
*   **Signals**: Nutrients (amino acids), Growth factors, Energy (AMPK).
*   **Outputs**: Promotes ribosome biogenesis, protein synthesis; Inhibits autophagy.
