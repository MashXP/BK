# Chapter 12: Population Genetics

## 1. Inheritance in Self-fertilizing Populations
In populations where individuals primarily self-fertilize (common in some plants), the frequency of heterozygotes decreases by half each generation.

### Genotype Frequencies at Generation $n$:
For a population starting with $100\%$ heterozygotes ($Aa$):
- **Genotype Ratio**: $(2^n-1)AA : 2Aa : (2^n-1)aa$
- **Effect**: As $n$ increases, the proportion of heterozygotes ($Aa$) decreases significantly while homozygotes ($AA$ and $aa$) increase, approaching pure homozygosity.

---

## 2. Inheritance in Random Mating Populations
In large, randomly mating populations, allele and genotype frequencies remain constant from generation to generation in the absence of evolutionary forces.

### Hardy-Weinberg Principle
The relationship between allele frequencies ($p, q$) and genotype frequencies:
- **Formula**: $p^2AA + 2pqAa + q^2aa = 1$
    - $p$: frequency of dominant allele $A$
    - $q$: frequency of recessive allele $a$
    - $p + q = 1$

### Conditions for Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium:
1. No mutations.
2. No natural selection.
3. No genetic drift (large population size).
4. No gene flow (migration).
5. Random mating.

---

## 3. Determining Population Equilibrium
- **Calculating T**: Used in specific equilibrium models (e.g., $T = P \times RH^2$).
- **Chi-square ($\chi^2$) test**: Used to determine if observed genotype frequencies significantly deviate from Hardy-Weinberg expectations.
