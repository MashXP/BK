# Chapter 9: Chromosome Genetics

## 1. The Nucleus: Genetic Library of the Cell
The DNA within the nucleus is organized into discrete structures called **chromosomes**.
- **Chromatin**: A mix of DNA and protein. In non-dividing cells, it appears diffuse.
- **Chromosome**: Condensed chromatin, formed when the cell prepares for division.
- **Structure**:
    - **Histones**: Proteins that DNA wraps around to form **Nucleosomes**.
    - **Solenoid Chromatin Fiber**: Further coiling of nucleosomes.
    - **Condensed Chromosome Components**:
        - **Arm**
        - **Centromere**: Essential for chromosome segregation; central component of the kinetochore (attaches and moves chromosome during mitosis/meiosis).
        - **Telomere**: Essential for the stability of chromosome tips; elongation relies on **Telomerase**.

## 2. The Cell Cycle
A typical mammalian cell growing in tissue culture has a generation time of ~24 hours.
- **Interphase**:
    - **G1 Phase (Gap 1)** (~10 hours): Synthesizes mRNA and proteins. Includes the **G1/S checkpoint**. Cells may pass to **G0 phase** if growth factors are limited.
    - **S Phase (DNA Synthesis)** (~9 hours): DNA duplication occurs.
    - **G2 Phase (Gap 2)** (~4 hours): Protein synthesis for cell division. Includes the **G2/M checkpoint**.
- **M Phase (Mitosis)** (~1 hour): Nuclear and cytoplasmic division.

### Regulation of the Cell Cycle
- **Cyclins**: Proteins whose concentration fluctuates during the cycle.
- **Cdks (Cyclin-dependent kinases)**: Enzymes that bind to cyclins to become active.
- **MPF (Maturation-Promoting Factor)**: A cyclin-Cdk complex that triggers the cell's passage past the G2 checkpoint into M phase. Cyclin is degraded after the checkpoint to reset the process.

## 3. Chromosome Variation and Inheritance

### Telomere Shortening and Aging
- **Mechanism**: As cells divide, telomeres shorten. Eventually, cell division stops (senescence).
- **Telomerase**: An enzyme that uses an **RNA Template** to extend shortened telomeres. DNA polymerase then completes the complementary strand.

### Linked Inheritance
- **Sex-linked Inheritance**: Genes located on sex chromosomes (e.g., X-linked traits).
    - **X-Inactivation**: In females, one X chromosome is randomly inactivated in each cell lineage during early embryonic development, forming a **Barr body**.
- **Linkage**: Genes located on the same chromosome.
    - **Complete Linkage**: Genes are always inherited together.
    - **Incomplete Linkage**: Genes can be separated by **crossing over** during meiosis.

### Extranuclear Inheritance
- **Plastid/Mitochondrial Inheritance**: Inheritance of DNA from organelles outside the nucleus (usually maternal).
- **Cytoplasmic Male Sterility (CMS)**:
    - **cytS**: Sterile cytoplasmic factor.
    - **Rf**: Nuclear restorer of fertility gene.

## 4. Chromosomal Mutations and Abnormalities

### Structural Mutations
- **Deletion**: Loss of a chromosomal segment.
    - **Terminal**: Loss at the end.
    - **Interstitial**: Loss in the middle.
- **Duplication**: Repetition of a segment. Often caused by misaligned crossover.
- **Inversion**: Reversal of segment orientation.
- **Translocation**: Movement of a segment between non-homologous chromosomes.
    - **Simple**: One-way transfer.
    - **Reciprocal**: Two-way exchange.
    - **Robertsonian Translocation**: Fusion of two acrocentric chromosomes, often losing a small fragment.

### Numerical Abnormalities (Aneuploidy)
- **Nondisjunction**: Failure of chromosomes to separate properly during meiosis or mitosis.
    - **Meiosis I Nondisjunction**: Results in `n+1`, `n+1`, `n-1`, `n-1` gametes.
    - **Meiosis II Nondisjunction**: Results in `n+1`, `n-1`, `n`, `n` gametes.
- **Common Diseases**:
    - **Down Syndrome**: Trisomy 21.
    - **Trisomy X (47, XXX)**.
    - **Double-Y Syndrome (47, XYY)**.
    - **Klinefelter Syndrome (47, XXY)**.
    - **Turner Syndrome (45, X)**: Monosomy X in females.

### Polyploidy in Plants
- **Autopolyploidy**: Doubling of own chromosomes (induced by **Colchicine**).
- **Allopolyploidy**: Result of hybridization between different species followed by chromosome doubling.

## 5. Karyotyping and Diagnostic Techniques
- **Karyotype**: An organized profile of a person's chromosomes.
- **Preparation**: Cells are stimulated to divide, treated with a hypotonic solution to swell, fixed, stained, and viewed under a microscope.
- **Comparative Genomic Hybridization (CGH)**: Detects deletions and duplications (especially in cancer).
    - Cancer DNA (Green) and Normal DNA (Red) are hybridized to metaphase chromosomes.
    - **Ratio > 1**: Duplication.
    - **Ratio < 1**: Deletion.
