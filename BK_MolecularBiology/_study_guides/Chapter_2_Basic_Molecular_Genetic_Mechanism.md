# Chapter 2: Basic Molecular Genetic Mechanism

### 1. DNA Stability and Flexibility
*   **Flexibility**: Allows for Denaturation, Mutation, Replication, Recombination, Crossing-over, and Mobile elements.
*   **Stability**: Maintained by Renaturation, Repair mechanisms, Semi-constitutive replication, and the Double helix structure.
*   **Denaturation vs. Renaturation**: DNA melting curve (Tm) depends on G-C content (higher G-C = higher Tm).

### 2. DNA Replication
*   **Mechanism**:
    1.  **Unwinding**: Helicase (ATP-dependent) separates strands.
    2.  **Priming**: Primase adds RNA primers.
    3.  **Elongation**: DNA Polymerase adds nucleotides (5' to 3').
        *   **Leading Strand**: Continuous synthesis.
        *   **Lagging Strand**: Discontinuous (Okazaki fragments).
    4.  **Ligation**: DNA Ligase joins fragments.
*   **Fidelity (Proofreading)**:
    *   **3'-5' Exonuclease Activity**: DNA Polymerase has an intrinsic ability to "proofread" by removing mismatched nucleotides immediately after incorporation, ensuring high replication accuracy.
*   **Key Enzymes**:
    *   **Helicase (e.g., DnaB, RecBCD)**: Unwinds DNA.
    *   **Topoisomerase**: Relieves supercoiling.
    *   **SSB (Single-strand binding proteins)**: Stabilize unwound DNA.
*   **Specialized Initiation (e.g., Lambda Phage)**:
    *   **O Protein**: Binds to the replication origin.
    *   **P Protein**: Binds to O and recruits host **DnaB helicase**.
    *   **Activation**: **DnaJ, DnaK, and GrpE** (heat-shock proteins/chaperones) are required to remove the P protein, thereby activating the DnaB helicase to begin unwinding.
*   **Prokaryote vs. Eukaryote**:
    *   **Origin (Ori)**: Single (Prokaryote) vs. Multiple (Eukaryote).
    *   **Shape**: Circular (Prokaryote) vs. Linear (Eukaryote).
    *   **Polymerases**: 1 main type (Prokaryote) vs. 3 types ($\alpha, \delta, \epsilon$ in Eukaryotes).
    *   **Primase**: Free (Prokaryote) vs. Associated with Pol $\alpha$ (Eukaryote).
    *   **Termination**:
        *   **Prokaryote**: Specific termination (Ter) sites and Tus protein.
        *   **Eukaryote**: Occurs when **Y-shaped replication forks (branches) meet**; involves the **End-Replication Problem** due to linear chromosomes, leading to **Telomere** shortening.

### 3. DNA Repair Mechanisms
*   **Base Excision Repair (BER)**:
    *   Removes damaged bases (e.g., via DNA glycosylase).
    *   AP endonuclease cuts backbone.
    *   DNA Pol $\beta$ and Ligase seal the gap.
*   **Double Strand Break Repair**:
    *   **Homologous Recombination (RecBCD Pathway)**:
        *   **RecBCD Complex**: A multifunctional enzyme used for initiating recombination.
            *   **RecB**: 3'-5' translocase and nuclease.
            *   **RecD**: 5'-3' translocase.
            *   **RecC**: Responsible for **CHI sequence recognition** (5'-GCTGGTGG-3').
        *   **Mechanism**: RecBCD unwinds and digests DNA until it hits a CHI site; it then switches its nuclease activity to generate a 3' single-stranded tail for RecA loading.
    *   **Non-homologous End Joining (NHEJ)**: Ligate broken ends (error-prone).

### 4. Mobile Elements (Transposons)
*   **Mechanisms**:
    *   **Class 1 (Retrotransposons)**: "Copy and Paste" (DNA -> RNA -> DNA -> Insert). Uses Reverse Transcriptase.
    *   **Class 2 (DNA Transposons)**: "Cut and Paste" (Excise -> Insert). Uses Transposase.
*   **Impacts**:
    *   **Advantageous**: New regulatory networks, evolutionary adaptation/diversity.
    *   **Deleterious**: Lethal insertions, mutational meltdown, somatic diseases (cancer).
*   **Mammalian Protection**:
    *   KRAB/KAP1 histone modification (methylation).
    *   APOBEC sequence editing (C to U - cytidine deaminases).
    *   PIWI proteins/piRNA (cleavage and methylation).
