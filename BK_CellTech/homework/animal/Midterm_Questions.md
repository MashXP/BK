## 1. Comparison: Primary Culture vs. Immortalized Cell Line

| Feature | Primary Culture (P) | Immortalized (I) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Anchorage** ⚓ | Anchorage dependent (must attach to grow). | Reduced dependence (can grow in agar or suspension). |
| **Inhibition** 🛑 | Contact inhibited (stops at a monolayer). | Reduced inhibition (can grow in thick patches/foci). |
| **Lifespan** ⏳ | Finite (30–50 generations before senescence). | Infinite (continuous growth). |
| **Genome** 🧬 | **Stable (Diploid).** | **Unstable (Aneuploid/Heteroploid).** |
| **Growth Factors** 🍼 | High requirement (needs plenty of serum). | Reduced requirement (needs less serum). |
| **Doubling Time** ⏱️ | Standard/Slow. | Reduced/Faster. |

> **Note on Genome:** Avoid the term "haploidy" for cell lines. They are usually **aneuploid** (abnormal chromosome count) or **heteroploid** (variable counts across the population).

## 2. SV40 Immortalization Mechanism

The SV40 Large T-antigen facilitates immortalization by bypassing cell cycle checkpoints through two primary targets:

*   **Targeting pRb:** The T-antigen binds to the Retinoblastoma protein (pRb). This action releases the transcription factor **E2F**, which acts as a "key" to unlock the expression of genes required for the **S-phase** (DNA synthesis).
*   **Targeting p53:** The T-antigen sequesters and inactivates **p53**. This prevents the cell from triggering G1 arrest or apoptosis (programmed cell death) in response to DNA damage, effectively bypassing critical quality control checks.

## 3. Applications of Animal Cell Technology

Animal cell technology is utilized across various fields for both research and industrial production:

*   **Viral Vaccines:** Modern production of vaccines (e.g., Polio, Flu) using cell lines like Vero or MDCK.
*   **Monoclonal Antibodies (mAbs):** Produced via hybridoma technology or recombinant CHO cells for treating cancer and autoimmune diseases.
*   **Recombinant Glycoproteins:** Manufacturing complex therapeutic proteins that require specific sugar patterns (glycosylation), such as **Erythropoietin (EPO)** or **clotting factors**.
*   **Biochemistry Research:** Investigating how normal cells function physiologically and biochemically in a controlled environment.
*   **Artificial Tissues:** Engineering bioengineered materials like **artificial skin** or experimental organs for transplantation and wound healing.
*   **Toxicology:** Testing the effects of new drugs or chemical compounds on specific human cell types to reduce animal testing.
