## 1. Phases of Culture
Understanding growth phases is essential for timing subculture and cryopreservation.
1.  **Lag Phase**: Adaptation, synthesis of growth factors.
2.  **Growth (Log) Phase**: Exponential increase in cell number (`N = N0 * 2^x`).
3.  **Stationary Phase**: Plateau due to nutrient depletion, waste accumulation, and confluence (contact inhibition). Death rate equals growth rate.
4.  **Decline Phase**: Cell death.

## 2. Subculture Protocol
*   **Purpose**: To maintain cells in the exponential growth phase.
*   **Steps**:
    1.  Remove spent medium.
    2.  Wash with PBS (buffer).
    3.  Add **Trypsin** (proteolytic enzyme) to detach cells.
    4.  Incubate.
    5.  Resuspend in fresh medium (inactivates trypsin).
    6.  Count and reseed.

## 3. Cryopreservation Principles
Preserving cells at very low temperatures to stop biological time.

### Freezing Injury
*   **Ice Injury (Rapid Cooling)**: Formation of intracellular ice crystals which damage organelles and membranes.
*   **Solute Injury (Slow Cooling)**: Water freezes outside cells -> Osmotic imbalance -> Dehydration and high salt concentration damage cells.
*   **Optimal Cooling**: Slow cooling (approx. 1°C/min) with a cryoprotectant to balance these risks.

### Protocol
1.  **Preparation**: Collect cells in exponential phase. Resuspend in medium with **Cryoprotectant** (e.g., DMSO or Glycerol).
2.  **Prefreezing**: Cool to -30°C to -70°C (e.g., in an isopropanol container/EPS rack).
3.  **Storage**: Transfer to Liquid Nitrogen (-196°C) or vapor phase (-135°C).
4.  **Thawing**: **Rapid** thawing at 35-37°C to prevent recrystallization.

### Benefits
*   Reduces genetic drift and morphological changes.
*   Prevents aging and senescence.
*   Inhibits differentiation.
*   Reduces contamination risk.
