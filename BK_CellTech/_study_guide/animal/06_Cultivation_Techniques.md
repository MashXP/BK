# Cultivation Techniques
**Date:** 2026-02-06
**Source:** AlCellTech.pdf (Slides 56-66)

## 1. Microcarrier Culture
Allows anchorage-dependent cells to be grown in suspension (bioreactors) by providing a surface on small floating beads.

### Characteristics
*   **Beads**: Dextran, cellulose, or plastic.
*   **Concentration**: e.g., Cytodex (2x10^5 beads/mL).
*   **Conditions**: Requires gentle stirring (50-70 rpm) to keep beads in suspension without shear damage.

### Types of Microcarriers
*   **Cytodex 1**: Dextran matrix, positively charged (attracts cells).
*   **Cytodex 3**: Coated with Collagen type I (mimics ECM).
*   **Cytopore**: Macroporous cellulose (cells grow inside pores, protected from shear).
*   **Cytoline**: High density, silica/polyethylene.

### Harvesting
Separating cells from beads can be challenging.
*   **Detachment**: Chelating agents (EDTA), Enzymes (Trypsin), Hypotonic shock.
*   **Separation**: Differential sedimentation (beads settle faster), Filtration (100µm mesh).

## 2. Cell Encapsulation
Enclosing cells within a semi-permeable membrane.
*   **Purpose**:
    *   **Immune Isolation**: Protects transplanted cells from the host immune system (antibodies/immune cells cannot enter).
    *   Allows diffusion of nutrients, oxygen, and therapeutic products (e.g., insulin).
*   **Types**:
    *   Solid capsules (Alginate).
    *   Membrane-coated solid/hollow spheres.
    *   Hollow silica/inorganic hybrids.

## 3. Hanging Drop Method
*   Used for generating 3D spheroids or Embryoid Bodies (EBs) from stem cells.
*   Gravity concentrates cells at the bottom of a droplet to promote aggregation.

## 4. Adherent Cell Subculture Protocol (Q15)
Anchorage-dependent cells are grown in horizontal T-flasks with a substrate surface.

### Standard Protocol
1.  **Grow to confluency:** Culture cells in a T-flat (horizontal orientation) until they occupy **~80% of the growth surface** (confluent). Do **not** wait for 100% — overcrowding inhibits growth.
2.  **Remove spent medium.**
3.  **Wash** with PBS to remove residual serum (which inhibits trypsin).
4.  **Add Trypsin** (proteolytic enzyme) to cover the cell layer.
5.  **Incubate at 37°C for 10 minutes:** Trypsin hydrolyses cell-substrate protein bonds → cells round up and detach.
6.  **Mechanical agitation:** Gently tap the flask to ensure complete detachment → cells enter suspension.
7.  **Collect cells:** Transfer entire cell suspension to a centrifuge tube.
8.  **Centrifuge** at low speed to pellet cells.
9.  **Wash to remove trypsin:** Resuspend in fresh medium (serum inactivates trypsin) or PBS if cells are sensitive.
10. **Count cells** using a haemocytometer (Neubauer chamber) with trypan blue dye exclusion.
11. **Re-seed** into a new flask at **2–5 × 10⁵ CFU/mL**.

## 5. Suspension Cell Subculture Protocol (Q16)
Non-adherent cells grow in vertical T-flasks; no enzyme detachment is needed.

### Standard Protocol
1.  **Monitor by colour:** Medium colour indicates cell density:
    - **Yellow/orange → red** (phenol red indicator): cells are consuming nutrients, actively growing.
    - **Purple:** Alkaline shift, cells not actively dividing or medium needs changing.
2.  **No trypsin required:** Cells are already in suspension.
3.  **Collect cells:** Aspirate entire suspension into a centrifuge tube.
4.  **Centrifuge** at low speed to pellet cells.
5.  **Wash:** Resuspend in fresh medium or PBS (if cells are sensitive) to remove waste metabolites.
6.  **Count cells** on a haemocytometer to determine density.
7.  **Re-seed** in a **vertical T-flat** at an appropriate density for the cell line.

## 6. Bioreactor-Scale Culture Process

### Inoculation
1.  **Pre-condition bioreactor:** Equilibrate and stabilize all culture parameters (pH, dissolved O₂, temperature) **before** adding cells.
2.  **Prepare inoculum:** Ensure cells are in **log (exponential) phase** and as a **single-cell suspension** (best physiological state).
3.  **Seed at 1/3 volume:** Add inoculum to **one-third** of the final culture volume; stir at the **lowest possible speed** to keep microcarriers in homogeneous suspension without shear damage.
4.  **Allow attachment** (for adherent cells on microcarriers): Let cells settle and adhere.
5.  **Add remaining medium:** Once attachment is confirmed, top up to full culture volume.

### Cell Detachment from Microcarriers
-   **Enzymatic:** Trypsin, Protease (cleave cell-substrate bonds).
-   **Chelating agents:** EDTA (removes Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ needed for integrin adhesion).
-   **Hypotonic shock:** Hypotonic medium causes cells to swell and detach.
-   Often a combination of methods is used.

### Separating Cells from Carriers
-   **Differential sedimentation:** Microcarriers settle faster than free cells.
-   **Filtration through 100 µm mesh:** Beads retained, cells pass through.
-   **Density-gradient centrifugation:** Separates by density differences.

### Cell Encapsulation
-   Cells are enclosed in **alginate capsules (microcapsules)**.
-   **Advantages:**
    -   Protects cells from high inhibitory metabolite concentrations in the bulk medium.
    -   Prevents attack by host immune cells (for *in vivo* delivery).
    -   Reduces accumulation of secondary/toxic by-products around cells.
