# Chapter 2: Plant Cell Culture Conditions

## 1. Plant Cell Culture Laboratory

The plant tissue culture laboratory consists of specialized rooms designed to maintain sterility and provide optimal growth conditions.

### 1.1. Media Preparation Room
- **Water Quality**: Must use **distilled water** (purified to remove minerals, gases, and organic matter).
- **Equipment**:
    - **Analytical Balances**: Used for measuring milligrams (e.g., plant hormones, vitamins).
    - **Precision Balances**: Used for measuring grams (e.g., macronutrients, agar, sucrose).
    - **Water Distiller**: For producing high-purity water.
    - **Autoclave**: For steam sterilization of media and equipment.
- **Culture Vessels**: Must be made of materials that do not release growth-inhibiting substances.

### 1.2. Transfer Room (Inoculation Room)
- **Environment**: A closed, controlled environment, often with glass doors to minimize airflow.
- **Laminar Flow Cabinet (Clean Bench)**:
    - **Pre-filter**: Removes large particles like dust and hair.
    - **HEPA Filter**: High Efficiency Particulate Air filter; removes $\ge 99.97\%$ of airborne particles (bacteria, viruses, fungal spores).
- **Sterilization**: Wiping surfaces with **76% alcohol** and using UV lamps/antifungal solutions.

### 1.3. Growth Room (Incubation Room)
- **Temperature**: Typically maintained at **$25 \pm 2^\circ\text{C}$**, though adjustable between $15-30^\circ\text{C}$ based on species.
- **Light Control**:
    - **Intensity**: Adjustable.
    - **Photoperiod**: Standard is **16h light / 8h dark**.
    - **Light Quality**: Controlled wavelength spectrum.

## 2. Basic Laboratory Procedures

### 2.1. pH Adjustment
- **Standard pH**: Most media are adjusted to **$5.8 \pm 0.02$** using NaOH or HCl.
- **Timing**: Must be adjusted **before autoclaving**.
- **Significance**: pH affects ion solubility, agar gelling ability, and cellular metabolic activity.

### 2.2. Sterilization Techniques
- **Facilities**: UV lamps and antifungal solutions for the transfer room.
- **Surfaces**: 76% alcohol for hoods and hands (up to elbows).
- **Metal Tools**: Sterilized using **fire/flaming**.
- **Media Sterilization**:
    - **Autoclaving**: $121^\circ\text{C}$ for 15-20 minutes (for heat-stable components).
    - **Filter Sterilization**: For heat-sensitive chemicals.

### 2.3. Surface Sterilization of Explants
1.  **Washing**: Use soapy water to remove soil and dust.
2.  **Rinsing**: 10-30 minutes under running water.
3.  **Disinfection**: Performed inside the laminar flow hood using specific disinfectants.
4.  **Final Rinse**: Multiple rinses with **sterile distilled water**.

### 2.4. Factors Influencing Sterilization Effectiveness
The goal of sterilization is to obtain a **sterile and living sample**. Several factors influence this:
- **Type of Explant**: Different tissues have varying levels of endogenous contamination and sensitivity to disinfectants.
- **Contamination Levels**: The initial microbial load on the tissue.
- **Disinfectant Concentration and Exposure Time**: Must be balanced to kill microbes without killing the plant tissue.
- **Aseptic Technique**: The skill and rigor of the operator in maintaining a sterile environment.

**Common Disinfectants**:

| Disinfectant | Concentration (%) | Exposure Time (min) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| Calcium hypochlorite | 9-10 | 5-30 |
| Sodium hypochlorite | 0.5-5 | 5-30 |
| Hydrogen peroxide | 3-12 | 5-15 |
| Ethyl alcohol | 70-95 | 0.1-5.0 |
| Silver nitrate | 1 | 5-30 |
| Mercuric chloride | 0.1-1.0 | 2-10 |

## 3. Plant Tissue Culture Medium

### 3.1. Medium Composition
#### 3.1.1. Macronutrients (N, P, K, Mg, S, Ca)
- Essential for cell division, differentiation, and organ formation.
- **Nitrogen**: Essential for amino acids, proteins, and nucleic acids; provided as $NO_3^-$, $NH_4^+$, or organic forms.
- **Magnesium**: Central component of the chlorophyll molecule.

#### 3.1.2. Micronutrients (Fe, Cu, Zn, Mo, Mn, B, Co, KI)
- Act primarily as **cofactors for enzymes**.
- **Iron (Fe)**: Usually provided in **chelated form (FeEDTA)** to prevent precipitation.

#### 3.1.3. Organic Supplements
- **Vitamins**: Thiamine (B1), Nicotinic acid (B3), Pyridoxine (B6); act as coenzymes.
- **Myo-inositol**: Stimulates cell growth ($100\text{ mg/L}$).
- **Amino Acids**: Glycine, asparagine, proline (organic nitrogen sources).
- **Carbon Source**: Sucrose is standard ($1-6\% \text{ w/v}$); necessary as in vitro plants have limited photosynthesis.

#### 3.1.4. Specialized Additives
- **Undefined Supplements**: Coconut water, yeast extract, malt extract, fruit juices (provide sugars, vitamins, and natural PGRs).
- **Activated Charcoal**: $0.5-3\text{ g/L}$; absorbs toxic compounds and PGRs; darkens medium to stimulate root formation.
- **Gelling Agents**: **Agar** (from red algae) is most common. Firmness depends on concentration and pH.

### 3.2. Plant Growth Regulators (PGRs)
- Organic compounds that control/modify growth at **very low concentrations**.

### 3.3. Medium Selection
- **Nutrient-Poor**: White, Knop.
- **Moderate-Nutrient**: B5 (Gamborg).
- **Nutrient-Rich**: **MS (Murashige and Skoog)**; the most well-balanced and widely used.
