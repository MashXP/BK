# Chapter 1: Introduction to Plant Tissue Culture

## 1. Principles of Cell Tissue Culture

### 1.1. Cell Theory
- **Core Tenets**:
    1. All living organisms are composed of cells.
    2. The cell is the basic unit of organisms.
    3. Cells arise from pre-existing cells.
- **Key Concept**: A single cell can regenerate and develop into an entire organism under appropriate conditions.

### 1.2. Totipotency
- **Definition**: Totipotency refers to the ability of a plant cell to differentiate into any cell type and develop into a whole plant.
- **Visual Process**: Leaf → Explant (small piece) → Callus → Plantlet.

### 1.3. Steps of Plant Tissue Culture
1.  **Explant Isolation**: A plant tissue (explant) is scraped or cut from the parent plant (mother plant).
2.  **Inoculation**: The explant is placed on a growth medium supplied with nutrients and phytohormones under **sterile conditions**.
3.  **Callus Formation**: The explant loses specificity and divides to form a mass of undifferentiated cells called **callus**.
4.  **Regeneration**: Over time, small plantlets begin to develop from the callus (Shoot regeneration → Root development).
5.  **Acclimation/Transplantation**: The plantlets are transplanted into pots or trays filled with nutrient-rich compost for acclimation.

## 2. Types of Plant Cell Culture

### 2.1. Organ Culture
- Cultivation of entire plant organs such as roots, shoots, or leaves.
- **Applications**: Production of secondary metabolites (Drugs, Food, Dyes, Cosmetics).
- **Example Species**: *Ginkgo biloba*, *Taxus wallichiana*, *Panax ginseng*, *Aloe vera*, *Vanilla planifolia*, etc.

### 2.2. Callus Culture
- Culture of non-differentiated cells formed from explants.
- Can be induced to develop into whole plants.

### 2.3. Cell Suspension Culture
- Individual cells cultured in a liquid medium (shaker flasks).
- **Value**: Large-scale production of plant metabolites and biomass production.

### 2.4. Protoplast Culture
- **Definition**: Protoplasts are cells with their cell walls removed.
- **Fusion**: Protoplasts can be fused to form hybrid cells with combined genetic traits.
- **Example**: **Pomato** (Hybrid of Tomato and Potato).

## 3. History of Plant Cell Tissue Culture

### Stage I (1902 - 1930): Discovery
- **1902 (Haberlandt)**: Cultured fully differentiated single cells from leaves. They survived for a month but failed to divide.
- **1932 (White)**: Successfully established continuously growing tomato root cultures (maintained until 1968).
- **Key Advancement**: Identifying the suitable explant.

### Stage II (1934 - 1954): Optimization
- **1934 (White)**: Cultured tomato root tips in medium containing salts, glucose, and yeast extract (later replaced by B vitamins).
- **1939 (Gautheret)**: Induced callus formation from the cambium layer of woody species using IAA (Indole-3-acetic acid).
- **1948**: Synthesis of synthetic auxins.
- **Key Advancement**: Recognition of the role of vitamins and plant growth regulators.

### Stage III (1957 - 1992): Hormonal Control & Media
- **1957 (Skoog and Miller)**: Discovered that the **ratio of kinetin (cytokinin) to auxin** affects organogenesis:
    - **High Auxin / Low Cytokinin**: Root-inducing.
    - **Balanced Ratio**: Callus-inducing.
    - **Low Auxin / High Cytokinin**: Shoot-inducing.
- **1962 (Murashige and Skoog)**: Formulated the **MS medium**, the most extensively used culture medium today.

### Stage IV: Application
- Broad application in plant propagation and various industrial fields.

---

## 4. Applications of Plant Tissue Culture
a. **Rapid Propagation**: Mass production of clones.
b. **Conservation**: Protecting endangered species (especially medicinal plants).
c. **Secondary Metabolites**: Production of cosmetics and pharmaceuticals.
d. **Synthetic Seeds**: Production of artificial seeds.
