# Mechanical and Thermal Unit Operations Study Guide

## I. Mechanical Unit Operations Group (Grinding - Sieving - Mixing)

### 1. Hammermill Grinding:
- Understand the structure of the hammermill and the feeding system (specifically the primary function of the screw feeder).
- Safe operating procedure: Start-up sequence, feeding method, and determining the end of the grinding time by continuously monitoring the indicating instruments (e.g., the ammeter returning to the no-load value).
- Grinding theories: Differentiating the physical principles and practical limitations of Rittinger's Surfacial Theory, Kick's Volumetric Theory, and Bond's Law.
- Grinding efficiency and error analysis: Distinguishing between objective equipment limitations (heat dissipation, particle dispersion) and subjective experimental errors (measurement and timing inaccuracies).

### 2. Sieving Analysis:
- Rules for assembling the sieving system (gradually decreasing mesh/aperture sizes from top to bottom, and the position of the solid collecting pan/receiver at the bottom).
- Data processing: Calculating the mass fractions retained on individual sieves, determining the representative particle size (arithmetic average of aperture sizes), and applying the sieving efficiency equation ($E$).

### 3. Solid Mixing:
- Application scope of the rotating drum mixer (highly suitable for dry, free-flowing granular solids or powders).
- Homogeneity evaluation: The random sampling procedure at specific locations inside the rotating drum, the practical significance and calculation of the Mixing Index ($I_s$), the sample standard deviation ($s$), and the theoretical ideal standard deviation ($\sigma_e$).

## II. Heat Transfer - Thermodynamics - Drying Group

### 1. Convective Drying:
- Thermodynamic concepts: Theoretical definition and physical meaning of the wet-bulb temperature.
- Drying kinetics: Analyzing the variation of drying rate and moisture content during the constant-rate drying period and the falling rate period. Applying mathematical equations to calculate drying time.
- Equipment characteristics: Principle of the non-recirculation convective dryer mode (where hot air passes through the material once and is completely vented out) and the procedure for accurately setting/investigating air temperatures.

### 2. Double Pipe Heat Exchanger:
- Experiment objectives: The method to determine the actual heat transfer coefficient and the significance of comparing it with theoretical values derived from empirical equations.
- Flow arrangements: Distinguishing the physical flow setups between parallel flow (cocurrent flow) and countercurrent flow.

### 3. Thermodynamics (Cooling & Dehumidification):
- Measurement principles: Techniques for accurately placing the sensor to measure air velocity, converting measured velocity to volumetric flow rate, and adjusting different air velocity regimes (settings).
- Energy & mass balance calculations: Utilizing moisture content to calculate the theoretical mass of condensed water, and using enthalpy to compute the cooling capacity of the cooler.

## III. Mass Transfer - Hydraulics Group (Distillation - Packed Column - RTD)

### 1. Distillation:
- Process parameters: Differentiating between directly measured parameters (temperatures, flow rates, concentrations) and calculated parameters (reflux ratio, number of ideal stages, overall column efficiency). Practical measurement techniques for distillate flow rate using a graduated cylinder and timer.
- Theoretical basis: Definition and operational nature of the reflux ratio.
- McCabe-Thiele method: Establishing the operating line equation for the rectifying (enriching) section and the stripping (exhausting) section, finding intersection points on the diagonal line, drawing the q-line (feed condition line), and determining the number of ideal stages.

### 2. Packed Column:
- Equipment characteristics: Configuration of control valves for gas/liquid flow rates (e.g., bypass and fine-tuning valves). Properties of common packing materials like Ceramic Raschig rings.
- Measurement & Calculation: Principles of reading a U-tube manometer. Analyzing the relationship and difference in pressure drop between a dry-packed column and a wet-packed column, and evaluating the dry-packing friction coefficient.

### 3. Residence Time Distribution (RTD):
- Objectives: Determining the actual Residence Time Distribution (RTD) function and comparing it with theoretical models (such as the tanks-in-series model).
- Method: Understanding the pulse injection stimulus-response technique and its mathematical representation using the Dirac Delta Function ($\delta(t)$).
- Calculations: Mastering equations for the theoretical mean retention time and the actual average retention time.
