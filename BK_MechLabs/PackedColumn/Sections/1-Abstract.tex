\section{ABSTRACT}

\subsection{Objective}
A Packed column was applied in mass transfer processes such as gas absorption, distillation, and liquid extraction. These experiments were to investigate the hydrodynamic characteristics and the effects of the gas and liquid velocities on the operating performance of the packed column. In the packed column, the inlet liquid is distributed over the top of the packed section and flows downward in the column, while the inlet gas was entered the distributing space below the packed section and flows upward through the interstices in the packing countercurrent to the flow of the liquid.

Through the experiments, the students could determine:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The effects of gas and liquid velocities on the pressure drop of gas flow in the packed column.
    \item The variation of the friction coefficient of dried packing ($f_{dry}$) as a function of Reynolds number ($Re$) of inlet gas flow rate and inferring the experimental relations.
    \item The change of "$\sigma$" factor, showing the relationship between the pressure drop of the gas flow through the dried packing and that through the wetted packing in different inlet liquid velocities.
    \item The diagram of limiting operation performance of packed column (loading and flooding points).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Experiment Method}
Gas flow is supplied with different flowrates to the packed column containing ceramic chocks. Pressure drop is surveyed in turn when only gas flow goes into the column (dried column) and when gas flow goes into the column combined with liquid flow going down from the top with different flowrates (wetted column).
