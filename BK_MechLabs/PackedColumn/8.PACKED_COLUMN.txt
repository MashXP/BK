# I. ABSTRACT:

## Objective: 

A Packed column was applied in mass transfer processes such as gas
absorption, distillation, and liquid extraction. These experiments were
to investigate the hydrodynamic characteristics and the effects of the
gas and liquid velocities on the operating performance of the packed
column. In the packed column, the inlet liquid is distributed over the
top of the packed section and flows downward in the column, while the
inlet gas was entered the distributing space below the packed section
and flows upward through the interstices in the packing countercurrent
to the flow of the liquid.

Through the experiments, the students could determine:

1\) The effects of gas and liquid velocities on the pressure drop of gas
flow in the packed column

2\) The variation of the friction coefficient of dried -- packing
(f~dry~) as a function Reynolds number (Re) of inlet gas flow rate and
inferring the experimental relations

3\) The change of "σ" factor, showing the relationship between the
pressure drop of the gas flow through the dried packing and that through
the wetted packing in different inlet liquid velocities

4\) The diagram of limiting operation performance of packed column
(loading and flooding points)

## Experiment method:

Gas flow is supplied with different flowrate to packed column containing
ceramic chocks. Pressure drop is surveyed in turn when only gas flow
goes into column (dried column) and when gas flow goes into column
combined with liquid flow goes down from the top with different
flowrates (wetted column)

# II. THEORY:

## Pressure drop of inlet gas flow:

The Pressure drop (∆P~dry~) of the gas flow through the packed column is
depended on the gas velocity (G) in the condition of dried -- packing
(no countercurrent-liquid flow). The pressure drop in the packed column
increases as raising the velocity of the inlet gas flowing through
interstices in the packing materials. The pressure drop increases with
the 1.8 -- 2.0 power of the velocity of the gas.

∆P~dry~ = α G^n^

Where in, n = 1.8 -- 2.0

When the liquid enters the column at the top of packing and flows
downward, the interstices in the packing are reduced, due to the
occupation of the liquid in the free volume of the packing material.
Therefore, the displacement of the gas flow was harder in the packed
column. With an increment of gas flow rate, the impedimental effect of
the liquid flow regularly increases. When the gas flow rate reaches a
critical value, the pressure drop rises gradually. If the gas glow rate
exceeds this loading point, the pressure drop increases more rapidly and
does not follow the equation (1). In the local regions of the column,
the liquid becomes a continuous phase, and the column reaches the
flooding point. The entire column may fill with fluid at this flooding
point.'

The graph of log(∆P~c~/Z), describing the pressure drop per unit packing
depth, is show in figure 1.

## The friction factor f~dry~ as a function of Re~c~ for dried column:

The pressure drop (∆P~dry~) of gas flow through the packed column with
no countercurrent-liquid flow can be determined using Ergun equation:

$$\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta}P}_{dry}}{Z}\frac{\varepsilon^{3}}{(1 - \varepsilon)}.\frac{d_{td}\rho_{G}}{G^{2}} = \frac{150(1 - \varepsilon)}{Re} + 1.75$$

Chilton and Colburn suggested a relation between pressure drop of gas
flow and gas velocity through the packed column

$${\mathrm{\Delta}P}_{dry} = 2f_{dry}\frac{G^{2}Z}{\rho_{g}D_{h}}\gamma_{h}\gamma_{w}\ ,\ N/m^{2}$$

Z: the paking depth, m

G: gas flow velocity per unit of packed section, kg/s.m^2^

D~h~: nominal size of packing material, m

ρ~g~: density of gas flow, kg/m^3^

γ~h~: the adjustment factor of packing material

γ~w~: the adjustment factor for effect of wall column on void fraction
of packed column

Sherwood collected the data of previous studies and suggested the
characteristic values for Raschig rings:

γ~h~ = 0.35

γ~w~ = 1

However, Zhavoronkov suggested another relation, referring the void
fraction, is more accurate for ∆P~dry:~

$${\mathrm{\Delta}P}_{dry} = \frac{2f_{dry}G^{2}Z}{\varepsilon^{2}\rho_{G}D_{e}}\ ,\ N/m^{2}$$

Wherein: ε: porosity of packing material

D~e~ = 4ε / a equivalent diameter of packing material, m

a: specific surface area of packing material, m^2^/m^3^

Friction factor f~dry~ is a function of dimensionless Re~c~ and is
determined using the following equation:

$${Re}_{c} = \frac{GD_{e}}{\varepsilon\mu} = \frac{4G}{a\mu}$$

µ: viscosity of gas flow, kg/ms

Zhavaronkov determined the variation of gas flow from laminar flow to
turbulent flow regime through the Re~c~ of 50. In turbulent flow region,
the Re~c~ is in the range of 50 \< Re~c~ \< 7000 with the random packed
column. The f~dry~ is calculated as following equation:

$$f_{dry} = \frac{3.8}{{Re}_{c}^{0.2}}$$

However, this generalized relation is not accurate because it doesn't
entirely mention the effect of packing material's shape.

## Pressure drop ∆P~wet~ for wetted column:

The correlation between the pressure drop for dried column ∆P~dry~ and
that for wetted column ∆P~wet~ can be described as following

∆P~wet~ = σ∆P~dry~

Therefore, we can estimate:

f~wet~ = σ f~dry~

Where σ is a coefficient as a function of the liquid velocity L,
kg/m^2^s

Leva suggested a relation showing the effect of L on σ:

σ = 10^ΩL^

or log σ = ΩL

The value of σ depends on types, dimension, packing mode (random or
regular mode) and liquid velocity L.

For example, with the Raschig rings for 12.7 mm, random packing, the
porosity ε of 0.568, the liquid velocity L from 0.39 to 11.7 kg/m^2^s,
the column is operated below the loading point:

Ω = 0.084

From some previous studies, the relation between the ratio of
$\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta}p}_{wet}}{{\mathrm{\Delta}p}_{dry}}$ and
coefficient of fluid irrigation can be showed as following

$$A = \sqrt{\frac{1.75}{{Re}_{L}}\left( \frac{G_{L}}{F\rho_{L}} \right)\frac{q}{2g\varepsilon^{2}}}$$

For A \< 0.3 and ceramic packing material with d \< 30 mm:

$$\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta}p}_{wet}}{{\mathrm{\Delta}p}_{dry}} = \frac{1}{(1 - A)^{3}}$$

$${Re}_{L} = \frac{4G_{L}}{Fa\mu_{L}}$$

## The flooding point of packed column:

When the packed column is flooded, the liquid seizes the packed section
entirely, and the flows are disordered drastically. This phenomenon is
atrocious for the operation of the packed column. The G~L~ which is
responsible for this flooding situation is called G~L~^\*^.

![](media/image5.png){width="2.377817147856518in"
height="1.6884503499562555in"}

Figure 2: Flooding plot of packed column

Zhavoronkov pointed out that the flooding situation appears when the two
number of π~1~ and π~2~ for the packed column have the relationship.

$$\prod_{}^{}1 = \left( \frac{f_{dry}.a}{\varepsilon^{3}} \right)\frac{v^{2}}{2g} \bullet \frac{\rho_{G}}{\rho_{L}} \bullet \mu_{tđ}^{0.2}$$

And

$$\prod_{}^{}{2 = \frac{L}{G}\sqrt{\frac{\rho_{G}}{\rho_{L}}}}$$

Where f~dry~: friction factor for dried column

v: the flow rate of gas before feeding to the packed column, m/s

µ~tđ~: relative viscosity of liquid compared to water

$\mu_{tđ} = \frac{\mu_{l}}{\mu_{water}}$, if the liquid is water µ~tđ~ =
1

Therefore, the correlation of π~1~ and π~2~ as showed in the plot of log
π~1~ -- log π~2~ determined the flooding graph of packed column. The
limiting operation of the packed column is under this plot.

# III. EXPERIMENTAL APPARATUS: 

1.  **Diagram of experimental apparatus**

The experimental unit consists of:

1)  A glass column where the Raschig rings are packed randomly.

2)  Gas feeding equipment with air blower (B~K~), gas pipe, U -- tube
    manometer differential pressure gauge and gas flowmeter with scale
    from 8 to 100%.

3)  Water feeding equipment with feedtank (N), water pump (B~L~) and
    liquid flowmeter with range from 0 to1.6.

<!-- -->

2.  **Characteristic of packed column**

Glass column:

-   Diameter d = 0.09 m

-   Height H = 0.805 m

-   Packing height Z = 0.42 m

Packing material which is Raschig rings is arranged randomly, with the
nominal diameter "d" = 12.7 mm, surface area "a" = 370 -- 380 m^2^/m^3^,
porosity "ε" = 0.586.

Figure 3: Diagram of experimental apparatus

List of components in the diagram of experimental apparatus:

-   A: Panel

-   C: Packed column

-   E: Bottom part of column

-   F~K~: Gas flowmeter, V = 0.286 m^3^/min

-   g: a liquid level device (pipe) at the bottom part of column

-   F~L~: Liquid flowmeter, G~L~ = 5.805 l/min

-   B~K~: air blower with a capacity of 1.0 Hp

-   B~L~: liquid pump with a capacity of 0.5 Hp

-   N: liquid tank

-   AK: pressure gauge

3.  **Experimental Method**

    a.  **Preparation**

-   Close all of the liquid valves

-   Turn on the air valve and switch the air blower in 5 min for
    removing all moisture in the column. Then, shut down the blower

-   Open valve "1" and valve "3", and pumping water into the bottom part
    of the column. Using valve "2" to adjust the water level equal to
    the height of level device (the height = $\frac{3}{4}$ of the bottom
    part of the column). Then, turn off the pump and close valve "3".

    a.  **Measurement of pressure drop for dried column**

-   Close all of the liquid valves. Open air valve "6" when the air
    valve "5" is closed. Turn on the air blower and open valve "5"
    slowly for adjusting the gas flow into the column

-   With a fixed gas flow rate, determining of P~dry~ on the pressure
    gauge for the mmH2O unit. After that, turn off the blower and
    waiting for 5 min

    a.  **Measurement for pressure drop of the wetted column**

-   Switch on the air blower and adjust gas flow rate into the column in
    range of 15 -- 20% for maximum flow rate

-   Open the liquid valve "1" and turn on the pump. Using valve "VL"
    from the liquid flowmeter to adjust the water flow rate at the top
    of the column. If the float in flowmeter does not pop up as the
    valve "VL" is opened at full volume, close the valve "1" closely to
    raise the liquid flow rate

-   At a specific liquid flow rate, altering the air flow rate and
    measuring the P~wet~ until the operation of the column reaches the
    flooding point and accomplishing the experiment

-   Raising the liquid flow rate and conducting the experiment as above

> Note:

1)  Adjust the valve "4" to maintain the height of water level equal to
    $\frac{3}{4}$ the height of the bottom part of the column through
    the measurement of the pressure drop

2)  Using the valve "2" to reduce water level from the column, if needed

3)  Turn off the water pump B~L~ before opening the valve "4" at full
    volume. Finally, switch off the air blower B~K~.

4)  If the water infiltrates into the gas feeding pipe, opening the blue
    valve behind the panel to eliminate water.

# V. DISCUSSION:

1.  Based on the chart and practical data, we have:

> Dry column: G increases, the $\mathrm{\Delta}P$ increases linearly.
>
> Wet columnL G increases, $\mathrm{\Delta}P$ increases obviously partly
> as separated areas as chart mentioned in the theory. When the liquid
> flowrate increases, column is closer to the flood point.
>
> After the loading point, $\mathrm{\Delta}P$ increases rapidly,
> suddenly. The line in this area becomes so inclined that it is
> difficult to operate the packed column at the bubble mode, although it
> is the best condition for the packed column to operate. In fact, when
> carrying out the experiment, till the flooding point, we stop the
> experiment, so there is no area after loading point on the chart.

2.  Purpose and the usage of the chart f according to Re.

> The chart f according to Re is built to express the dependence of the
> restriction force into the flowrate of the fluid. If the fluid
> flowrate increases, the friction factor f between two phases
> increases. The chart is built to determine the suitable flowrarte to
> operate the column with the small restriction force and get the best
> mass transfer efficiency between two phases without making the column
> reach the flooding point. Nonetheless, the observation showed from the
> chart is opposite, which means when the flowrate increases, the
> restriction force reduces gradually. This result is due to the effect
> of the error during the experiment.
>
> If one of both values Re or F is given, it can use the chart to
> determine another: From the given f or Re, draw a straight line
> horizontally or vertically, cut the chart f_Re at one point. From that
> intersection point, draw a perpendicular line to the other axis, then,
> missing value is determined.

3.  Does the relation between the analyzed objectives follow the
    prediction? If not, explain:

> The relation between the objectives are approximately closer to the
> prediction. Particularly, the relation is expressed as following:

-   Log(∆P~ck~/Z) and logG are dependent linearly together following the
    theoritical statement above.

-   ∆P~cö~/Z and G are nearly separated into two distinguished areas:
    The area under the loading point and the area above the loading
    point.

> \+ The area below the loading point: $\mathrm{\Delta}P$ increases
> slowly and gradually, so these points are virtually lie on one line.
>
> \+ The area above the loading point: $\mathrm{\Delta}P$ increases
> rapidly, suddenly, so the line is very inclined; If increasing the
> liquid flowrate and gas flowrate, the column will gradually reach the
> flooding point.

-   Logσ and L: Completely depends linearly together, so expressed as a
    line on the chart.

4.  Nevertheless, during the experiment, some factors can cause the
    error.

-   Pump makes the liquid flowrate unstable.

-   Fan makes the liquid flowrate unstable.

-   The water column maintained at the bottom of the column is not
    sufficient as the requiremnt, which leads to the flooding in the
    difference pressure tube affecting the result.

-   Error in reading and experiment implement.

-   The experiment conditions are different among the measurement.

*Note:*

After adjusting the gas flowrate and liquid flowrate, the data must be
collected immediately.

During the experiment, it needs to take 10 minutes to cool down the
apparatus and stabilize the system, then continue the operation.

If the water runs in unconcidentally into the gas tube, open the valve
after the board.
