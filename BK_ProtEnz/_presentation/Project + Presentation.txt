Isolation and Evaluation Methods
Bacterial isolation requires a defined inorganic salt base where the target pesticide serves as the sole carbon and
energy source, selecting against non-degrading oligotrophs (Gangola et al., 2018; Hajri et al., 2026; Boufercha et
al., 2022; Zheng et al., 2026). Standard mineral salts medium (MSM) formulations utilize low-cost analytical
reagents consisting of $(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{SO}_4$ or $\text{NH}_4\text{Cl}$, $\text{K}_2\text{HPO}_4$,
$\text{KH}_2\text{PO}_4$, $\text{NaCl}$, $\text{MgSO}_4\cdot 7\text{H}_2\text{O}$, and trace iron salts
adjusted to neutral pH (Gangola et al., 2018; Kannan et al., 2024; He et al., 2022). Isolates are screened by enriching
contaminated environmental samples in liquid MSM followed by plating on 1.5% agar matrices supplemented with
the pesticide (Gurjar & Hamde, 2020; Zheng et al., 2026; He et al., 2022).

Industrial Protease Processing
Industrial applications of microbial proteases, predominantly sourced from Bacillus and Aspergillus species, rely on
large-scale fermentation operations to replace aggressive chemical processing (Razzaq et al., 2019; Solanki et al.,
2021; Singh & Bajaj, 2017). Production takes place in stirred-tank bioreactors utilizing fed-batch or chemostat
fermentation systems, where parameter control must be maintained to maximize catalytic yield (Razzaq et al., 2019;
Solanki et al., 2021). Downstream extraction and recovery frequently utilize ultrafiltration systems, which serve as
pressure-driven separations that minimize activity loss while removing low-molecular-weight contaminants
(Razzaq et al., 2019).

 • Bioreactor and processing configurations deploy standardized stirred-tank fermenters and downstream

   membrane units to control microbial cultivation and subsequent catalytic hydrolysis (Razzaq et al., 2019; Solanki
   et al., 2021).
 • Purification and immobilization for reuse overcome thermal instability and autoproteolysis through dialysis,

   ultrafiltration, and carrier-binding immobilization, allowing biocatalysts to be retained and recycled across
   successive reaction batches (Razzaq et al., 2019; Song et al., 2023).
 • Mechanical and chemical stress endurance is high in wild-type and recombinant Bacillus proteases, which

   demonstrate poly-extremotolerance to broad temperature and pH regimes, organic solvents, and industrial
   inhibitors (Solanki et al., 2021; Singh & Bajaj, 2017).


Cypermethrin Toxicity Thresholds
A concentration of 100 g/kg (100,000 mg/kg) of cypermethrin substantially exceeds microbial tolerance limits and
induces acute ecological toxicity (Gangola et al., 2018; Rehman et al., 2024; Kannan et al., 2024). In biodegradation
literature, pure bacterial strains and consortia—such as Bacillus subtilis 1D, Bacillus cereus, and Pseudomonas—
demonstrate upper tolerance limits between 150 mg/L and 500 mg/L, with 450 to 500 mg/kg representing the
typical maximum survival threshold (Gangola et al., 2018; Kannan et al., 2024; Gurjar & Hamde, 2020).

In edaphic systems, exposure to cypermethrin at 1,000 mg/kg already suppresses microbial biomass carbon by
86.2% and drives a 77% drop in soil respiration rates (Rehman et al., 2024). Consequently, applying 100 g/kg
creates complete microbial inhibition, far exceeding the physiological processing capacity of transforming soil
microorganisms (Gangola et al., 2018; Rehman et al., 2024).
