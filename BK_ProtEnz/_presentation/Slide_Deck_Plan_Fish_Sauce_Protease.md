### [SLIDE 3: INDUSTRIAL CONTEXT & THE AUTOLYTIC BOTTLENECK]
* **Title:** The Traditional 18-Month Bottleneck: Endogenous Protease Collapse
* **Layout:** Split Hero (Industrial Problem + Biochemical Root Cause)
* **Text:**
  * **Kicker:** INDUSTRIAL BOTTLENECK
  * **Headline:** Endogenous Enzymes Undergo Rapid Autolysis and Salt Inactivation in Brine
  * **Content:**
    * *Commercial Burden:* Traditional fermentation locks working capital in solar vats for 12–18 months.
    * *Biochemical Root Cause:* Endogenous visceral trypsins and chymotrypsins suffer rapid self-digestion (autolysis) and osmotic dehydration in 20–25% NaCl.
    * *Engineering Objective:* Exogenous, salt-stable proteases must accelerate liquefaction from months to days.
  * **KPIs:**

    * `20–25% NaCl`: Mandatory food safety salinity barrier

### [SLIDE 4: SUBSTRATE MATRIX BIOCHEMISTRY]
* **Title:** The Insoluble Substrate Matrix: Actomyosin & Collagen Stroma
* **Layout:** Two-Card Structural Comparison
* **Text:**
  * **Kicker:** SUBSTRATE BIOCHEMISTRY
  * **Headline:** Dense Myofibrillar Bundles and Triple-Helical Stroma Resist Simple Digestion
  * **Content:**
    * *Myofibrillar Complex (65–75%):* Filamentous actin-myosin polymers tightly locked by hydrophobic inter-filament packing.
    * *Stromal Matrix (3–10%):* Triple-helical tropocollagen ($[\text{Gly}-\text{Pro}-\text{Hyp}]_n$) reinforced by covalent pyridinoline cross-links.
    * *Enzyme Selection Rule:* Proteases must exhibit low $K_m$ and high catalytic turnover ($k_{\text{cat}}/K_m$) on insoluble fibrous proteins, not just soluble casein.
* **Visual:** Structural diagram contrasting filamentous actomyosin bundles against the rigid triple-helical collagen fibril.

### [SLIDE 5: CORE CATALYTIC MECHANISM: THE SERINE TRIAD]
* **Title:** Catalytic Machinery: The $\text{Ser}-\text{His}-\text{Asp}$ Charge-Relay Network
* **Layout:** Process Flow (Active-Site Electron Transfer Cascade)
* **Text:**
  * **Kicker:** CATALYTIC MECHANISM
  * **Headline:** General Base Activation Converts Serine into a Potent Alkoxide Nucleophile
  * **Content:**
    * *Resonance Barrier:* Peptide bonds possess $\sim 40\%$ double-bond character; uncatalyzed half-life $t_{1/2} \approx 500\text{ years}$.
    * *Charge Relay:* $\text{Asp102}$ immobilizes and polarizes $\text{His57}$; $\text{His57}$ acts as general base to deprotonate $\text{Ser195}$.
    * *Nucleophilic Attack:* The resulting $\text{Ser195}-\text{O}^-$ attacks the scissile peptide carbonyl carbon, initiating cleavage.
  * **Exemplar Protease:** Alcalase / Subtilisin Carlsberg (EC 3.4.21.62).
* **Visual:** 2D chemical mechanism diagram showing the $\text{Asp102}-\text{His57}-\text{Ser195}$ catalytic triad and proton transfer.

### [SLIDE 6: TRANSITION-STATE STABILIZATION: THE OXYANION HOLE]
* **Title:** Transition-State Stabilization: The Oxyanion Hole Engine
* **Layout:** Split Hero (Oxyanion Architecture + Free Energy Impact)
* **Text:**
  * **Kicker:** TRANSITION-STATE COMPLEMENTARITY
  * **Headline:** Backbone Amide Hydrogen Bonds Lower Activation Energy by $10^6$-Fold
  * **Content:**
    * *Tetrahedral Intermediate:* Attack generates a short-lived, negatively charged $sp^3$ tetrahedral adduct ($T_1$).
    * *Oxyanion Hole Anatomy:* Dual hydrogen bonds from backbone amides (Asn155 and Ser195 in Subtilisin) coordinate the negative oxygen ($\text{O}^-$).
    * *Acyl-Enzyme Formation:* Collapse of $T_1$ expels the free amine product, leaving a covalent acyl-enzyme intermediate ready for hydrolytic release.
* **Visual:** Close-up active-site schematic showing the oxyanion hole caging the tetrahedral oxygen atom with dual hydrogen bonds.

### [SLIDE 7: SUBSITE SPECIFICITY & THE BITTERNESS TRAP]
* **Title:** Subsite Specificity (Schechter & Berger) & The Bitterness Trap
* **Layout:** Process Flow ($S_1\text{--}S_4$ Subsite Binding $\to$ Oligopeptide Release)
* **Text:**
  * **Kicker:** ACTIVE-SITE SPECIFICITY
  * **Headline:** Hydrophobic $S_1$ Pocket Binding Yields Intensely Bitter Peptides
  * **Content:**
    * *Subsite Mapping:* Substrate residues $P_4\text{--}P_4'$ dock into complementary enzyme pockets $S_4\text{--}S_4'$.
    * *Alcalase $S_1$ Preference:* Large, hydrophobic cleft binds apolar residues (Leu, Phe, Tyr, Val).
    * *The Bitterness Trap:* Uncontrolled Alcalase endoproteolysis generates 500–3,000 Da oligopeptides with exposed hydrophobic termini.
* **Visual:** Binding pocket diagram showing a hydrophobic $P_1$ residue docked inside the Alcalase $S_1$ pocket, releasing bitter oligopeptides.

### [SLIDE 8: SENSORY BIOCHEMISTRY: NEY'S Q-RULE & DEBITTERING]
* **Title:** Sensory Remediation: Ney's Q-Rule & Aminopeptidase Debittering
* **Layout:** Split Hero (Thermodynamic Q-Rule + Enzymatic Remedy)
* **Text:**
  * **Kicker:** SENSORY BIOCHEMISTRY
  * **Headline:** Exopeptidases Shave Hydrophobic Termini to Abolish Bitter Receptor Binding
  * **Content:**
    * *Ney's Q-Rule Threshold:*
      $$Q = \frac{\sum \Delta f}{n} \quad (\text{Threshold: } Q > 1,400\text{ cal/mol} \implies \text{Intense Bitterness})$$
    * *Debittering Solution:* Supplement with salt-tolerant fungal aminopeptidases (e.g., *Aspergillus oryzae* / Flavourzyme).
    * *Mechanistic Action:* Selectively cleaves terminal hydrophobic residues, liberating non-bitter free amino acids (Leu, Ile) and umami glutamate.
* **Visual:** Hydrophobicity curve showing bitter threshold ($1,400\text{ cal/mol}$) dropping below detection upon aminopeptidase treatment.

### [SLIDE 9: ENDO- VS. EXO-PEPTIDASE SYNERGISTIC KINETICS]
* **Title:** Dual-Enzyme Synergy: Driving Viscosity Drop and Umami Yield
* **Layout:** Process Flow (Sequential Digestion Cascade)
* **Text:**
  * **Kicker:** ENZYMATIC SYNERGY
  * **Headline:** Endopeptidases Multiply Free Termini for Rate-Limiting Aminopeptidases
  * **Content:**
    * *Endopeptidase Role:* Rapidly liquefies bulk mash, collapsing viscosity by cleaving internal polypeptide loops.
    * *Exopeptidase Role:* Processively clips terminal ends, driving Amino Nitrogen to Total Nitrogen ($A_N/T_N > 50\%$).
    * *Experimental Validation:* Alkaline protease alone reaches $\text{DH} \approx 29.1\%$; adding *A. oryzae* aminopeptidase boosts $\text{DH} \to 49.8\%$.
  * **Citation:** Zhao et al. (2026).
* **Visual:** Digestion cascade schematic: Myofibril Mesh $\to$ [Endo Cleavage] $\to$ Oligopeptides $\to$ [Exo Trimming] $\to$ Free Glu/Asp.

### [SLIDE 10: BIOPHYSICS OF HALOTOLERANCE: SURVIVING 20–25% NaCl]
* **Title:** Biophysics of Halotolerance: Structural Integrity in 4 M Brine
* **Layout:** Two-Card Structural Comparison (Mesophile vs. Halotolerant)
* **Text:**
  * **Kicker:** OSMOTIC STABILITY
  * **Headline:** Acidic Surface Residues Form a Coordinated Electrostatic Hydration Shell
  * **Content:**
    * *Mesophilic Failure (Papain, Bromelain):* 20–25% NaCl strips hydration water; induces active-site collapse and precipitation (70–90% activity loss).
    * *Halophilic Adaptation (*B. subtilis*, *B. megaterium*):* Surface enriched in acidic carboxylate residues ($\text{Asp}^-, \text{Glu}^-$).
    * *Biophysical Mechanism:* Acidic side chains coordinate structured $\text{Na}^+-\text{H}_2\text{O}$ dipoles, preserving catalytic fold geometry in saturated brine.
  * **Citations:** Fu et al. (2008); Chau et al. (2021).
* **Visual:** Electrostatic surface potential comparison: Neutral mesophilic surface (dehydrated) vs. Red-shaded acidic surface (hydrated in 4 M NaCl).

### [SLIDE 11: SALT-INDUCED KINETIC MODULATION & PROGRESS MODELING]
* **Title:** Reaction Kinetics Under Salt Stress: The Logarithmic Progress Model
* **Layout:** Split Hero (Thermodynamic Penalty + Logarithmic Curve)
* **Text:**
  * **Kicker:** KINETIC MODELING
  * **Headline:** Low Water Activity ($a_w \approx 0.75$) Slows Turnover and Follows Logarithmic Decay
  * **Content:**
    * *Water Activity Effect:* Saturated brine reduces free water activity, increasing the transition-state barrier:
      $$\Delta \Delta G^\ddagger = -RT \ln\left(\frac{k_{\text{cat, salt}}}{k_{\text{cat, 0}}}\right) \implies K_m \uparrow, \quad k_{\text{cat}} \downarrow$$
    * *Valdivia Progress Equation:*
      $$P(t) = \frac{1}{b}\ln(1 + abt)$$
      *(where $a$ = initial rate constant tied to active enzyme dose; $b$ = slowdown from substrate exhaustion and autolysis).*
  * **Citation:** Valdivia et al. (2025).
* **Visual:** Hydrolysis progression curves fitted to logarithmic model comparing commercial enzyme doses ($4.2, 8.4, 16.8\text{ U/g}$).

### [SLIDE 12: THERMAL DYNAMICS & CALCIUM AUTOLYSIS PROTECTION]
* **Title:** Enzyme Structural Dynamics: Autolysis Protection & Calcium Loops
* **Layout:** Two-Card Structural Feature (Self-Cleavage vs. Calcium Capping)
* **Text:**
  * **Kicker:** STRUCTURAL STABILIZATION
  * **Headline:** High-Affinity $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ Binding Sites Lock Subtilisin Fold Against Autolysis
  * **Content:**
    * *Autolysis Mechanism:* At industrial reaction temperatures ($50\text{--}55^\circ\text{C}$), active protease molecules digest exposed flexible loops of neighboring enzymes.
    * *Calcium Coordination:* Conserved $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ loops in Subtilisin coordinate divalent cations via backbone carbonyls and Asp carboxylates.
    * *Thermal Locking:* $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ binding raises thermal denaturation temperature ($T_m$) by $>15^\circ\text{C}$, preventing loop unfolding during 48h hot liquefaction.
* **Visual:** 3D structural model of Subtilisin highlighting the calcium ion coordination sphere stabilizing the active-site scaffold.

### [SLIDE 13: PROCESS ARCHITECTURE: THE TWO-STAGE STAGED MODEL]
* **Title:** Process Architecture: Decoupled Enzymatic Liquefaction & Microbial Aging
* **Layout:** Process Flow (Two-Stage Decoupled System)
* **Text:**
  * **Kicker:** PROCESS BIOENGINEERING
  * **Headline:** Decoupling Rapid Proteolysis from Microbial Aroma Maturation Slashes Time by 75%
  * **Content:**
    * *Stage 1 — Accelerated Liquefaction:* $50\text{--}55^\circ\text{C}$, 7–10% salt or halotolerant cocktail; complete physical mash liquefaction in 24–72 h.
    * *Stage 2 — Brine Maturation:* Brine brought to 20–25% NaCl, ambient $30\text{--}35^\circ\text{C}$, inoculated with *Tetragenococcus halophilus* (60–90 days).
    * *Flavor Rationale:* Authentic fish sauce volatile bouquet (esters, volatile fatty acids) requires microbial metabolism that pure proteases cannot synthesize.
  * **Citations:** Lê et al. (2015); Gao et al. (2020).
* **Visual:** Block diagram: `[Raw Fish Mash] --> [Stage 1: 50 deg C Enzyme Liquefaction] --> [Brine Up to 25%] --> [Stage 2: Microbial Aroma Cellar]`.

### [SLIDE 14: FOOD SAFETY MECHANISM: HISTIDINE DECARBOXYLASE SUPPRESSION]
* **Title:** Safety Imperative: Suppressing Bacterial Histidine Decarboxylase
* **Layout:** Split Hero (Biochemical Mechanism + Regulatory Thresholds)
* **Text:**
  * **Kicker:** FOOD SAFETY & TOXICOLOGY
  * **Headline:** Maintaining Salinity and Thermal Limits Inhibits Biogenic Amine Synthesis
  * **Content:**
    * *The Toxic Pathway:* Pelagic fish (anchovy) release abundant free histidine. Wild *Morganella morganii* express pyridoxal-phosphate (PLP)-dependent **histidine decarboxylase**, producing toxic histamine.
    * *Inhibition Mechanism:* Maintaining salt $>15\%$ or applying immediate $50\text{--}55^\circ\text{C}$ heating halts microbial decarboxylase gene expression.
    * *Regulatory Thresholds:* FDA $<50\text{ ppm}$ | Codex Alimentarius $<200\text{ ppm}$.
  * **Citation:** Taoka et al. (2019).
* **Visual:** Reaction equation ($\text{Histidine} \xrightarrow{\text{Decarboxylase}} \text{Histamine} + \text{CO}_2$) alongside microbial growth boundary chart.
