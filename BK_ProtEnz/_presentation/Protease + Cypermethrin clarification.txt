Microorganisms actively mineralize cypermethrin by cleaving its ester linkage, degrading intermediate metabolites
such as 3-phenoxybenzaldehyde, and opening aromatic rings to yield non-toxic molecules (Kansal et al., 2023;
Huang et al., 2018).


Industrial Protease in Fish Sauce Fermentation

In the fish sauce industry, manufacturing traditionally requires mixing fresh fish with 30% to 40% sea salt and
fermenting the biomass for up to two years (Bai et al., 2025). Because traditional processing produces undesirable
flavor metabolites, protracted processing times, and elevated biogenic amines, external proteolytic systems and
specialized bacterial starter cultures are introduced to optimize liquefaction (Bai et al., 2025; Lê et al., 2015). In
                              ướ     ắ
Vietnamese fish sauce ("n c m m"), metagenomic profiling reveals that Tetragenococcus halophilus dominates
fermentations, utilizing prolidases and aminopeptidases alongside Bacillus and Staphylococcus proteases to
release free amino acids under extreme salinity (Vu et al., 2026).

Exogenous and microbial proteases significantly shorten processing durations from years to days or months (Bai et
al., 2025; Fu et al., 2008; Nakano et al., 1986). Inoculation with Bacillus zhangzhouensis produces comparable
volatile profiles in 7 days while substantially decreasing toxic biogenic amines relative to 2-year traditional sauce
(Bai et al., 2025). Similar accelerated proteolysis occurs using salt-tolerant acid proteases from Bacillus megaterium
KLP-98, marine Pseudomonas proteases, and psychrotolerant Planococcus maritimus XJ2, though high sodium
chloride concentrations generally reduce the relative rate of peptide bond cleavage (Gao et al., 2020; Lê et al.,
2015; Fu et al., 2008; Nakano et al., 1986). Combining starter cultures like Tetragenococcus halophilus with
commercial proteases similarly accelerates protein recovery from fish processing by-products and reduces total
volatile basic nitrogen (Yang et al., 2025).


Cypermethrin Degradation Pathways
Bacterial and fungal isolates eliminate cypermethrin through sequential enzymatic actions that detoxify both the
parent compound and downstream aromatic intermediates (Kansal et al., 2023; Kaur & Singh, 2021; Saied et al.,
2021; Hu et al., 2018). The initial step relies primarily on carboxylesterase or esterase enzymes that cleave the
central ester linkage, splitting cypermethrin into cyclopropane carboxylic acid derivatives and unstable
cyanohydrins (Kansal et al., 2023; Pankaj et al., 2016; Kannan et al., 2024).
