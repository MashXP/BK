### 2. Protein

#### Secondary Structures

**$\alpha$-helix: Amino acids and helix formation**

* Alanine (Ala) and leucine (Leu) help form $\alpha$-helices.


* Proline (Pro) often breaks the helix because its rigid structure disrupts normal hydrogen bonding.


* Glycine (Gly) can also break the helix because it is too flexible.


* Interactions between side chains that are 3–4 residues apart can make the helix more or less stable.


* Hydrophobic amino acids placed every 3–4 residues can create a helix with one hydrophobic face.



---

**$\alpha$-helix: Helix dipole**

* Each peptide bond has:


* A partially negative carbonyl oxygen


* A partially positive amide hydrogen




* Their alignment creates a large dipole across the helix.


* The N-terminus is partially positive, while the C-terminus is partially negative.


* Therefore, negatively charged amino acids are often found near the N-terminus.
*(Diagram note: Shows dipole alignment with $\delta+$ at Amino terminus and $\delta-$ at Carboxyl terminus)*



---

**$\beta$ Sheets**

* Peptide bonds and $\alpha$-carbons give $\beta$-sheets a pleated shape.


* Hydrogen bonds connect neighboring $\beta$-strands.


* Side chains point alternately above and below the sheet.
*(Diagram note: $\beta$ strand side view showing alternating side chains above and below)*



---

**Types of $\beta$ Sheets**

* **Parallel:** Strands run in the same direction. The H bonds are bent and weaker. Repeat period: $6.5\text{ \AA}$.


* **Antiparallel:** Strands run in opposite directions. The H bonds are straighter and stronger. Repeat period: $7\text{ \AA}$.



---

**$\beta$-Turns**

* $\beta$-turns help the protein chain change direction.


* Each turn usually contains four amino acids.


* A hydrogen bond stabilizes the turn.


* Proline is common at position 2, and glycine at position 3.
*(Diagram note: Chemical structures of Proline and Glycine)*



---

**$\beta$ Sheets: Proline Peptide Bonds**

* Almost all peptide bonds are in the *trans* form.


* About 6% of peptide bonds involving proline are in the *cis* form; cis-proline bonds are common in $\beta$-turns.


* Proline isomerases help convert cis and trans forms.
*(Diagram note: Shows structural equilibrium between trans and cis proline isomers)*

#### Tertiary Structure

**Definition & Stabilization**

* The overall three-dimensional shape of a single polypeptide chain.


* Stabilized by interactions between amino acid side chains, including:


* Hydrophobic interactions


* Hydrogen bonds / Ionic interactions


* Van der Waals forces


* Strong disulfide bonds may also stabilize the structure.





---

**Folding & Classification**

* Interacting amino acids may be far apart in the primary sequence but close together after protein folding.


* Based on the shape, proteins are commonly classified as:


* **Fibrous proteins:** long, strong, and usually insoluble


* **Globular proteins:** compact and usually water-soluble

#### 3.3. Enzyme Kinetics

##### 3.3.2. Derivation of the Michaelis–Menten Equation

**Step 1: The initial rate of product formation is:**

$$v_0 = \frac{d[\text{P}]}{dt} = k_{\text{cat}}[\text{ES}] \quad (1)$$

To determine $v_0$, the concentration of the enzyme–substrate complex $[\text{ES}]$ must first be expressed in terms of the substrate concentration $[\text{S}]$ and the total enzyme concentration $[\text{E}]_T$.

---

**Step 2: The Steady-State Assumption (*giả định trạng thái ổn định*)**

* The steady-state assumption states that the concentration of the $\text{ES}$ complex remains approximately constant during the initial-rate measurement:


$$\frac{d[\text{ES}]}{dt} \approx 0$$



$$\Rightarrow \text{Rate of ES formation} = \text{Rate of ES breakdown}$$



* The rate of $\text{ES}$ formation is: $k_1[\text{E}][\text{S}]$

* The rate of $\text{ES}$ breakdown is: $k_{-1}[\text{ES}] + k_{\text{cat}}[\text{ES}]$


Thus:

$$k_1[\text{E}][\text{S}] = (k_{-1} + k_{\text{cat}})[\text{ES}] \implies [\text{E}][\text{S}] = \frac{k_{-1} + k_{\text{cat}}}{k_1}[\text{ES}] \quad (2)$$

The Michaelis constant is defined as:

$$K_m = \frac{k_{-1} + k_{\text{cat}}}{k_1} \quad (3)$$

From (2) and (3), therefore:

$$[\text{E}][\text{S}] = K_m[\text{ES}] \implies [\text{ES}] = \frac{[\text{E}][\text{S}]}{K_m} \quad (4)$$

---

**Step 3: Apply Enzyme Mass Conservation (*bảo toàn enzyme*)**

* The total enzyme concentration is the sum of the free enzyme and the substrate-bound enzyme:


$$[\text{E}]_T = [\text{E}] + [\text{ES}] \implies [\text{E}] = [\text{E}]_T - [\text{ES}]$$




Substituting this expression into the previous equation (4) gives:

$$[\text{ES}] = \frac{([\text{E}]_T - [\text{ES}])[\text{S}]}{K_m} \quad (5) \implies K_m[\text{ES}] = [\text{E}]_T[\text{S}] - [\text{ES}][\text{S}]$$

$$\implies K_m[\text{ES}] + [\text{ES}][\text{S}] = [\text{E}]_T[\text{S}] \implies [\text{ES}](K_m + [\text{S}]) = [\text{E}]_T[\text{S}]$$

##### 3.3.2. Interpretation of the Michaelis–Menten Equation

**Effect of Substrate Concentration**

**1. At low substrate concentration:**

$$\text{When } [\text{S}] \ll K_m \implies K_m + [\text{S}] \approx K_m \implies v_0 \approx \frac{V_{\max}}{K_m}[\text{S}]$$

* Under these conditions, the reaction rate is approximately proportional to $[\text{S}]$.


* The reaction is therefore first-order with respect to the substrate.



**2. When $[\text{S}] = K_m$:**

$$\text{When } [\text{S}] = K_m \implies [\text{S}] = K_m \implies v_0 = \frac{V_{\max}K_m}{K_m + K_m} = \frac{V_{\max}}{2}$$

* Thus, $K_m$ is the substrate concentration at which the initial rate is half of $V_{\max}$.



**3. At high substrate concentration:**

$$\text{When } [\text{S}] \gg K_m \implies K_m + [\text{S}] \approx [\text{S}] \implies v_0 \approx V_{\max}$$

* At this point, the enzyme is saturated with substrate.


* Increasing $[\text{S}]$ further has little effect on the velocity.


* The reaction is effectively zero-order with respect to the substrate.

  $$[\text{E}]_T = [\text{E}] + [\text{ES}] \implies [\text{ES}] = \frac{[\text{E}]_T[\text{S}]}{K_m + [\text{S}]} \quad (6)$$
* **Step 4:** Rate equation & saturation:
  $$v_0 = \frac{k_{\text{cat}}[\text{E}]_T[\text{S}]}{K_m + [\text{S}]} = \frac{V_{\max}[\text{S}]}{K_m + [\text{S}]} \quad (V_{\max} = k_{\text{cat}}[\text{E}]_T)$$
* **Regimes:**
  1. $[\text{S}] \ll K_m \implies v_0 \approx \frac{V_{\max}}{K_m}[\text{S}]$ (1st order in $[\text{S}]$, catalytic efficiency $= \frac{k_{\text{cat}}}{K_m}$).
  2. $[\text{S}] = K_m \implies v_0 = \frac{1}{2}V_{\max}$ (operational definition of $K_m$).
  3. $[\text{S}] \gg K_m \implies v_0 \approx V_{\max}$ (0th order in $[\text{S}]$, active sites saturated).
